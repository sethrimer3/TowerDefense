# Tower Defense

A mobile-first city-defense game. You lay out a walled city on a 9 × 13 board, then hold it against waves that keep coming until the keep falls. The Mine produces metals for city elements and upgrades; the Library produces Knowledge for research. Smiths work timed metal upgrades; researchers work timed Knowledge upgrades. Canvas draws the board; plain DOM provides the pages. No backend, no game engine, no framework; progress is kept in `localStorage`.

Play it on GitHub Pages: <https://sethrimer3.github.io/TowerDefense/>

## Run

Use Node.js 22.18+ (CI uses 24) and npm:

```sh
npm install
npm run dev      # http://127.0.0.1:5173/
npm test
npm run build    # dist/
npm run preview
```

## How to play

- **Areas.** Every 20 waves brings another area, beginning with the original Mossbound Ruins: desert, embers, flooded temple, mushrooms, frost, crystals, obsidian, stars and Nadir follow, then the cycle repeats after wave 200. Each area has a themed enemy roster; Nadir allows every normal wave enemy. Frozen Vault includes ships with frozen wakes, fire-vulnerable Ice Golems and orthogonally sliding Ice Cubes that leave meltable ice trails. Ground and city walls fade between areas over three seconds. The Wave selector uses each area's colors. Dry areas have no rain, the cold area snows, and the flooded temple is frequently rainy. Reduce Motion makes the change immediate and hides falling rain/snow.

- **City.** Drag city tiles, barracks, archer barracks, Mage Guilds, Valkyrie palaces, dark wizard keeps, monster bait, city gates, wall spikes, wall ballistas and archer, cannon, watch and wizard towers from the palette onto the gold-outlined tiles. The button at the palette's head picks what it shows: All, Towers, Infantry or City. City tiles can go anywhere off the top row, and a tile carries its buildings when dragged; each group of tiles gets its own wall. The keep is placed the same way, carrying its own tile, and tapping it while building opens its upgrade tree: Bastions (more HP), Arrow loops (it shoots) and Boiling oil (it scalds whatever reaches its walls), climbed side by side with Knowledge. Placed things can be dragged elsewhere or back to the palette. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag open ground to pan.
- **Start the defense.** The Wave button beside it picks the wave to start on (any up to your best), listing each wave's difficulty. Waves roll in without stopping (a short breather after each) until the keep falls. Every 10th wave is a boss wave fought at night. Civilians rebuild what the enemy smashes; bombs (bought in the Armory) can be dragged onto the field mid-battle, and the war banner (never used up) can be planted anywhere to rally every troop to it; tap it to take it down. A double tap anywhere on the board plants a free blue rally flag instead, which troops march to without the banner's bonuses; the flag and the banner replace each other, and a double tap on the flag takes it away (a Gameplay setting asks for a triple tap instead). Troops only answer either inside their deployment range: each troop building's troops stay within a circle round it (wider for stronger troops, and the Smithy's Deployment range widens them all). Tap a troop building in battle, then press it again and drag to set its own rally point, where its troops wait when there is nothing to fight; drag it onto a watch tower to send a gunner hall's gunners up it. The Necromancy strike spell (also never used up, ready again 30 seconds after each cast) raises every enemy that fell within 3 cells of where it lands as a friendly undead warrior for a minute. The Meteor strike (ready again 40 seconds after each cast) calls down a meteor that blasts every enemy within 2.5 cells of where it lands, sparing your own people. The Frost nova (ready again 35 seconds after each cast) freezes every enemy within 3 cells solid and leaves rime that slows them. Reinforcements (ready again 45 seconds after each cast) drop four soldiers out of the sky who hold the ground where they land for 30 seconds.
- **Wizard towers** alternate a flamethrower, which lights up the ground around it, and a wave of ice shards that chills (slows) everything it crosses.
- **Guns need a clear line of sight**: the whole gunner line (pistols, muskets, machine guns, the Gatling, rockets, snipers and laser snipers) shoots flat, so it never fires through a wall stone or building; archers, stone throwers and javelins arc over. A gunner whose hall's rally flag is dragged onto a watch tower climbs it while it has room (two a tower) and shoots over everything from the top, out of the enemy's reach, until the tower falls.
- **Buildings stand where you put them, on quarter tiles**: each city tile is a 4 × 4 grid of quarter tiles, and a building takes a fixed block of them (towers and monster bait 1 × 1, the guilds, Assassins' den, Spearmen hall, Phalanx, Javelin camp, Lancer stable, Gunner hall, Sniper lodge, Machine-gun nest, Gatling emplacement, Rocket battery, Revolutionary and Archer barracks 2 × 2, the Musket hall 3 × 2, the Barracks, Hero and Mage Guild 2 × 3, the Valkyrie palace 3 × 3, the Dark wizard keep 6 × 6 across four tiles). Buildings need a clear quarter between them; monster bait, walls and the wall's defenses may touch. An upgrade that changes a building's size sends it back to the palette.
- **The spearmen line.** A Melee Guild can become a Spearmen hall (spearmen who strike from a spear's length), which becomes a Phalanx (shielded hoplites who shrug off much of every blow), a Javelin camp (javelineers throwing heavy javelins) or a Lancer stable (fast riders whose first thrust at each enemy is a charge). Each has two paths of its own, and the Lancer stable's Winged lancers are crowned into the Valkyrie palace, the line's ultimate.
- **The Hero.** A Barracks can become the Hero, the swordsmen's ultimate (100,000 Knowledge): one hero who hunts the whole city and cuts through crowds of small enemies with sword combos, two sweeping slashes and then a spin that strikes everything round it. Its paths: Whirlwind (more, wider and harder spins), Blade dance (slashes that dash from enemy to enemy, kills adding more) and Legend (more HP, healing kills, rising once when it falls).
- **Valkyrie palaces** take a whole tile (half with Folded halls) and train armoured valkyries, who charge-stab: a golden blink down a line that hurts every enemy on it, stopping at buildings and the wall, after which nothing can hurt her for a second. Long spears lengthens the charge. The palace's Cloudborne path floats it on clouds: it drifts west across the sky, out of every enemy's reach, coming round from the east at another height, its valkyries dropping from wherever it floats, and it turns almost see-through over the city or anyone below.
- **C4 nanobots.** Cannon towers maxed on different branches merge into C4 nanobots, the cannon tower's ultimate, a 3 × 3 bunker that very slowly, from very far, fires harmless shells bursting into clouds of nanobots. Every enemy in a cloud is infected and explodes when it dies, so crowds go up in chains. Its shared upgrades, all of which it can take: a bigger blast, a bigger cloud, and a 1% to 5% chance a dying enemy sets free a new cloud.
- **Dark wizard keeps** fill a 2 × 2 block of city tiles (a single tile with the pricey Folded sanctum). Four corner turrets hurl black lightning that chains through up to 5 enemies, and the keep summons one dark wizard, the ultimate unit, whose bolts chain through 50 enemies packed close together (up to 250 with Conduit of night; Arc span lets them leap further).
- **City gates** go in the wall, on the edge of a city tile where the wall runs: a gatehouse whose doors swing open to let your troops and townsfolk out and in, and stay barred against the enemy, who must batter them down. While the doors stand open the enemy can slip in too. Every gate shares one upgrade tree (hold or tap one while building): Portcullis (bars the enemy while it is open, then murder holes and boiling pitch), Gate towers (small ballistas on the gatehouse) and Ironbound (more HP, mending, rising again after it falls); the Smithy's Ironbound doors and Greased hinges toughen it and shut it sooner. A gate whose edge stops being wall (a city tile added across it, or its own tile taken away) goes back to the palette.
- **Wall spikes** line one city tile's stretch of wall with iron-shod stakes pointing out, cutting every enemy on foot pressed against them. **Wall ballistas** stand on bastions at the wall's corners, only where it turns at a right angle, and shoot long bolts that pierce a file of enemies. They share one upgrade tree: Heavy bolts (harder, staggering, piercing eight), Fire bolts (burning, burning ground, bursting) and Windlass (quicker, further, a fan of three bolts), and the Smithy's Steel bolts, Torsion skeins and Long stocks raise their damage, reload and reach.
- **Monster bait** is a stack of crates that draws every enemy: while any stands, each enemy goes for the nearest one before the keep (fliers, siege engines and boats too), and houses lure nobody. A fallen stack stays fallen unless Restocking lets civilians rebuild it (once a level), and with Powder kegs it bursts as it falls and sets the ground round it burning.
- **Mage Guilds** train red fire mages who roam the streets hurling fireballs: each bursts with splash damage and leaves the ground burning, scorching whatever walks through.
- **Rewards and currency.** DEFEND awards exactly one **upgrade point** for each new highest wave cleared, with no payout for kills or replayed waves. The Mine makes **Copper**, **Silver** and **Gold**: every ten bars worked by its smiths make one spendable unit. These metals buy tiles, bombs, Smithy upgrades, Mine workers and buildings, and Library shelves, staff and lab expansions. Early purchases use Copper; later tiers add Silver and Gold. **Knowledge** comes from the Library and buys Study research.
- **Upgrades live where they are made.** The Mine's **Smithy** improves shared stats and equipment for every card of a type. The Library's **Study** researches paths once for your collection. In Defend, placing a building with a researched path asks which it becomes (or leave it unspecialized); tap it while building to change its path free. Buildings of one kind can wear different paths in the same defense; recruits inherit their home building's specialization. Buy in Tiles, improve in the Smithy, unlock in the Study, choose in Defend. Shared skills remain under Realm, City, Mine and Library.
- **Tiles tab.** Everything you own to put into a defense is a tile, in a hall of the keep under its great window: City tiles and the wall's pieces, Barracks, Towers, and Skills (bombs, and the war banner and strike spells, never used up). Each tile shows the Knowledge upgrades it carries: a diamond for each building it became, a pip in its path's colour for each rank, and its path's badge. Buildings upgraded one at a time are spread, a tile a copy, unless stacked, when copies with the same upgrades share a stack; City tiles, walls and skills are always one stack (×4). Tap a tile to open its kind in place and see each set of upgrades in words (in the city or ready), buy another, jump to its Smithy or Study topic, and see its higher tier. Filter by type, or open the Shop to buy any kind. The Defend header's Tiles button opens it.
- **Strike spell upgrades.** Each strike spell has its own topic under Spells: the Smithy raises its risen warriors' HP and damage, and the Study offers paths, cast one at a time (Bone archers shoot from afar; Soul weighing raises each warrior as strong as the enemy it was; Amalgam fuses all the fallen into one giant). The Meteor strike's Smithy rows raise its damage and blast radius; its paths are Starfall (a shower of 3, 5 or 7 fragments), Molten core (a burning crater, clinging at III) and Frost comet (chills, then a wider blast, then freezes). The Frost nova's Smithy rows lengthen its freeze and rime and widen it; its paths are Shatter (the frozen take more damage, then burst into shards as they thaw, then also when killed), Wandering blizzard (the rime hunts the nearest enemy, then grows and refreezes, then bites harder) and Rime ward (your people in the rime take less damage, then your buildings, then troops heal). The Reinforcements' Smithy rows make the soldiers tougher, harder hitting and slower to leave; its paths are Hammerfall (each lands with a shockwave, then staggers what it hits, then a champion lands with a quake), Muster the levy (seven soldiers, then nine with bowmen, then each fallen one replaced once) and Oathsworn (a shield wall, then kills keep the squad longer and heal, then a last stand).
- **Bomb research.** Study → Spells → Bombs has a connected Knowledge tree for Gunpowder I, II and III, adding 20% bomb damage per rank. It needs no troop or tower research. Existing ranks carry over. The Smithy’s Spells → Bombs holds damage training and Shaped charges; War drums is under Realm → All troops.
- **War banner research.** Open Study → Spells → War banner for its standalone Knowledge tree, with no tower-research prerequisite. Select a connected node to see its cost and research it. The banner has a 10-second placement cooldown, reduced to 7, 4 and 1 second through research. Shared upgrades add 10% absolute defense and enlarge its influence radius from 5 to 7 cells. Three independent branches add 25% damage within influence, 50% speed while marching toward it, or 25% life within influence followed by 5% maximum-life regeneration per second. The Skills palette shows cooldown fill and a gold outline when ready.
  - **Smithy rows** (in the Smithy): every ten copper, silver or gold bars the mine's smiths work make a Smithy point of that metal. A quadratic metal bill buys one rank of a row (troop HP and damage, drill speed, tower damage and reload, bomb damage, wall and keep HP, rebuild speed): copper for a row's first ten ranks, then silver, then gold. A smith works the rank on the wall clock (60 × next-rank² seconds per smith); more smiths on it share the time, and a smith on a rank stays at the smithy until it's done.
  - **Skills** (in the Study): ranked, paid with Knowledge at the start of a timed alchemy-lab project. All researchers share one project, taking 300 × next-rank² seconds of researcher work. No researchers pauses it; cancelling refunds its paid cost. Cancel research asks first; click Confirm cancel to discard progress and receive the refund, or Keep researching (or Escape) to continue. Paths use the same timer and evolution crowns take 24 researcher-hours.
  - **Paths** (in the Study, drawn as a tree from the building): the Wizard tower's Pyromancy (flames only, hotter), Rime (ice only, then freezing solid) or Stormcalling (chain lightning in place of flames); the Barracks' Crusaders (more HP, healing), Berserkers (faster strokes, kills heal, then cleaving blows) or Halberdiers (longer reach, slowing blows, then heavy blows on big enemies); the Valkyrie palace's Cloudborne (floats on clouds out of reach, then valkyries landing with a charge, then a bigger, quicker host); the Sniper lodge's Marksmen (reach, toughest first, then headshots), Ricochet (bullets glancing on to more enemies) or Suppressing fire (hits that slow, then stagger, then quicker reloads), Deadeye (much harder, further at III) or Piercing rounds (straight through every enemy in line, each hit softer); the Assassins' den's Bloodletters (huge criticals, less HP), Shadowcloak (unseen until they strike, ambushing from the cloak), Venomblades (poison that makes enemies take more damage from everything, spreading at III) or Headhunters (the toughest enemy first, with a bounty of its HP); the Musket hall's Sharpshooters (reach, harder balls, the toughest first, crowned into a Sniper lodge), Grenadiers (bursting balls that leave the ground burning, then throw bomblets, then clinging fire), Volley fire (extra balls at the next nearest, then quicker reloads in ranks) or Bayonets (a thrust at close quarters, then a citywide charge, then staggering); the Gunner hall's Quickdraw (faster reloads, then a second pistol) or Duelists (harder, further, every 3rd shot critical); the Machine-gun nest's Belt feed (faster reloads and harder bullets, crowned into a Gatling emplacement) or Tripod (reach and harder bullets); the Gatling emplacement's Gun shield (more life, a quicker carriage), Overdrive (all damage, no push) or Stopping power (bullets knocking enemies back, for little damage); the Rocket battery's Cluster rockets (wider bursts, bomblets, then burning ground) or Salvo racks (faster reloads, two then three rockets a launch); the Archer tower's Fire arrows (burning, then a volley), Hawk focus (highest-current-HP lock, consecutive-shot damage and speed ramps, then greatly extended range) or Wisp Arrow (living blue arrows with quiet trailing tassels and wide slices; 30 hits each, blue sparks on exhaustion and one refill every 3 seconds; Triple arrows raises blue capacity from one to three; Fire arrows adds two flaming arrows, each with 10 direct hits, lingering fire trails and a fiery explosion, regenerating one every 6 seconds; Lightning arrow adds one larger purple arrow that pierces straight to the map edge, leaving five-second lightning at 300% attack per second, then regenerates after 9 seconds); the Cannon tower's Gun crews (faster, grapeshot) or Siege shot (slower, harder, wider); the Archer barracks' Rangers (sight, heavier arrows, twin shots) or Skirmishers (faster draws, shooting on the move); the Watch tower's Spotters (marked enemies take more damage, wider marking) or Signal fires (marked enemies slowed, then burning); the Mage Guild's Stormcallers (long-range chain lightning and criticals), Pyromancers (larger fire blasts, burning ground and clinging fire) or Cryomancers (freezing blasts and stronger, longer slows); Monster bait's Oil-soaked (biters catch fire) or Fortified crates (more HP, then biting back); Wall spikes' Blasting stakes (blow up on contact, then rearm), Spring stakes (the whole row shoots out at everything in front of it) or Rimed stakes (chilling cuts, harder on the chilled, then chilling all nearby). Ranked paths have three upgrades in order; Wisp Arrow has its initial attunement, Triple arrows (I), Fire arrows (II) and the final Lightning arrow (III). Paths remain independently researchable; each placed building wears one, at its furthest researched rank. Researching a crown lets you evolve copies, one at a time and free, into a Dark wizard keep or Valkyrie palace (in the Study or Tiles), and return them to their base form free. Base copies remain for sale.
  - All Smithy purchases need smith work: ordinary levels take 120 × next-level² seconds per smith and share workers with the rows. Upgrades apply after completion, from the next defense. Prices increase quadratically; income grows through investment in Mine and Library capacity. See [the estimates](docs/UPGRADE_TIMING.md) for all ranks and the roughly two-year focused endgame targets.
- **Settings.** Reduce motion, the grass and water effects, the Defend board's tile grid lines (off by default, with an opacity slider when on), dev options (ALL ON, unlimited money, all towers, instantaneous research, all research per tab, adding an hour of idle time, and unlocking 100 more starting waves), and erasing progress.

The city is alive between and during battles: park grass sways in the wind (harder in the rain) and parts around anyone walking through it; ponds, trees, roofs and dirt streets are drawn as pixel art, with grass growing unevenly over the street edges and crisp shadows cast from a height map (the city wall's falling across the roofs beside it); trees stand over the units and fade while anyone is under them; ponds reflect their banks, trees and houses and ripple faintly all over when it rains, and in dry weather a few ducks paddle, dabble, preen and shy away from passers-by; lanterns, braziers and hand torches light the streets under cloud and at night (and when the keep falls, crumbling through four damaged stages to rubble, they go out one by one in a wave from it), and outside the walls the flagstones have a bump map, so every fire, blast and ice wave catches their edges. In Mossbound Ruins a forest fills that open ground: the crowns of large to huge oaks and pines seen from above, swaying in the wind over the parks' waving grass, a few small red-berried bushes, mossy rocks, bare dirt pads and a few glinting lakes, and any attack landing under the smaller trees strikes them apart into leaves and splinters, leaving a stump (sawn clean by blades); blasts blow any tree up into a crater, fire sets any alight to burn down to a pile of ash that those walking through push aside, and every blast and patch of burning ground bares the grass to dirt (the biggest trees take the biggest blasts). Boulders, rock clusters and small rocky mountains stand among the trees, and those do block the enemy and your troops on foot.

The Cannon Tower has three behavior-changing attunements: **Gun Battery** adds grapeshot immediately, **Mortar Tower** leaves burning ground (damage upgrades strengthen both the impact and the burn), and **Minelayer** constantly lays proximity mines at half range. Minelayer upgrades unlock aiming in battle (tap the tower, then press it again and drag), then raise capacity from 15 to 30 to 50 and add walking **Smart charges**. Drag the selected crosshair back onto its tower to restore random coverage. The three completed branches merge into C4 Nanobots.

The **Mage Guild** trains three attunements of troops: **Stormcallers** cast chain lightning from 9–15 simulation cells away, striking 3/4/6/8 enemies with 10/15/25/35% double-damage critical chance; **Pyromancers** combine larger fire blasts with full-area burning ground and clinging fire; **Cryomancers** freeze and slow an area, with three upgrades strengthening their reach, area and control. Existing Cinder Hall copies become Frost Hall/Cryomancers at their saved ranks. [Mage Guild attunement details](docs/upgrades/MAGE_GUILD_ATTUNEMENTS.md).

Each new furthest wave reached automatically selects the preceding wave for your next defense: reaching wave 10 starts your next run on wave 9, with a minimum of wave 1. Old saves with the former wave-1 default move to this starting wave when migrated. Use the starting-wave picker to replay earlier waves; that choice stays selected until you reach a new record. Reached-wave progress is saved separately from cleared waves and upgrade-point rewards.

## How it's built

Startup shows the Library's torch-lit stone with an animated wall torch on the left. In the bottom right, the loading dots appear in sequence while the keep's brick health bar builds one falling brick at a time. The lightweight boot entry loads the game behind this screen, then opens profile setup or welcome-back after the wall completes. Reduce Motion keeps the torch and dots still and skips the falling animation.

First entry asks for a player name and a custom pixel banner. The flag editor offers 16 colors and 12 stamp patterns, up to 12 layers with 25/50/75/100% stamp opacity, recoloring, reordering, removal and undo. The profile is saved on the device and displayed in Siege; Edit profile opens the editor again. Existing players without a profile keep their progress while completing setup.

The combat navigation button cycles Defend → Ambush → Siege when pressed while selected; returning from another tab keeps the current mode. Icons slide and fade, respecting Reduce Motion. All three modes use Defend's actual combat engine, graphics, towers, infantry, projectiles and spells. Siege retains the player's worn upgrades; Ambush starts from base stats and will buy its own upgrades with level gold. Ambush opens a three-column selector with 30 levels across ten zones, best-star ratings and sequential unlocks. Existing clears migrate to one-star Mossbound completions. Level cards lazily preview the same seeded native maps used in battle, with zone-specific ground and an arrow at every entry. Tracks wind down from the board's edges; from the tenth level enemies come in by two lanes, and from the twenty-second by three. Some levels have a river across them, crossed only by plank bridges where the roads meet it. Buildings now place freely on Defend's quarter-grid with its clear-space rule and native drag controls; water, roads, rocks and the castle area are reserved. The five base building types are available to everyone, independently of Defend progress. Each level has 20 native zone waves of enemies, crowds of 50 rising to 500, a little tougher each level, with bosses at 10 and 20, two fenced outposts and a stone-walled keep. Ground enemies raze outpost houses, while fliers head directly for the keep. Keep survival and remaining outposts determine 1–3 stars; losing the keep defeats the level. Send each wave manually or enable Auto-send. Level gold starts at 800 and grows a little each level; unlocked buildings can be bought repeatedly, including during waves. Base prices estimate potential DPS, rounded to tens; each wave's kills share gold in proportion to its enemies' HP. Select a building for its native local upgrades and damage statistics. Selling refunds every invested coin until wave 2 starts, then 70% rounded down to tens. [Cost estimates and economy rules](docs/design/AMBUSH_ECONOMY.md). [Scripted balance results](docs/design/AMBUSH_BALANCE.md). Siege has a dedicated thin page over the native two-sided battle. Its building targets use native pathfinding to expose reachable faces after wall breaches and flanks. Siege places every owned infantry building along the bottom against a saved native castle above, with real houses, walls, gates, towers, guards and a keep. Castles contain native paths, Armory levels, training, Skills and side-tree ranks, built through Defend's upgrade rules within a measured DPS budget; combat uses those upgrades directly. Native garrisons train and replenish as in Defend; destroying the enemy keep wins, with a four-minute limit. Siege's data-driven rank ladder has seven ranks, five divisions each, and procedural pixel emblems. New players start at Gold III (1000 MMR); Grandmaster I begins at 2720. Siege opens on the player's rank and Find Match. Opponent cards reveal a saved handle, stamped flag, rank, MMR and native castle preview before Attack. Back leaves the preview without a loss. Board name plates show both profiles and ranks; results show the rating change and promotions/demotions. Opponents use the same visible MMR scale, with no source badge. Rated results update MMR once; completed targets can be practiced. Reloading or forfeiting a started rated raid counts as a loss. Native castle-strength tools now measure every building path and rank across five real upgrade bands, with source-hash freshness checks and legal floor/ceiling blueprints. [Measurement protocol and limitations](docs/design/SIEGE_DPS.md). Siege strike spells use battle-local mana: start with 1, gain 1 per enemy troop killed up to 8, and spend 1 for Frost nova or Necromancy, 2 for Meteor, or 3 for Reinforcements. Their native effects and aiming are unchanged; Defend and Ambush retain cooldowns. Native scripted armies calibrate the rank curve with separate training and validation seeds; checked-in outcomes describe those offline armies, not measured player success probabilities. [Rating model and future player castles](docs/design/SIEGE_MATCHMAKING.md). Both modes share Defend's renderer, camera gestures and upgrade UI, pause offscreen and preserve your Defend city and inventory. Their controls sit above the battlefield; buildings are placed and selected directly on the board. Ambush saves best stars; its separate Star tree spends those stars on starting gold, bounties, building prices, refunds, damage/HP, fortifications, spell cooldowns and free starting towers. Undo and respec return stars instantly. Battle layouts restart on reload.

The Ultimate Archer Tower merges fully upgraded Fire, Hawk and Wisp towers. Idle allies feed its time field, suspending 30/60/90 physical shots until enemies enter range; it releases them in a rapid 45°/20°/0° spray. Independent branches unlock a 50–250-hit Divine Arrow reserve for incompatible damage and expensive 2×/5×/10× amplification. The golden arrow pierces to the map edge, resetting its damage reserve on launch. [Rules and tuning](docs/upgrades/ULTIMATE_ARCHER_TOWER.md).

Settings includes a saved language picker with nine native-name buttons in three columns: English, Spanish, French, German, Brazilian Portuguese, Italian, Japanese, Korean and Simplified Chinese. This first pass translates Settings, navigation and main controls; longer game descriptions still use English. Changes apply without a reload or ending a defense. [Coverage and translation guide](docs/LOCALIZATION.md).

The layout follows the browser’s visible viewport, including changes from browser controls and the keyboard, and keeps navigation above the device’s safe areas. Portrait and landscape layouts resize without a fixed minimum screen height.

The Wisp Tower's arrow chains broad, heading-preserving attack passes (`src/defend/wisp-flight.ts`), with a minimum turning radius of 24% of its range. Six quiet tassels trace recorded flight positions, hold their longer attack length for one second, then gradually shorten (`src/defend/wisp-art.ts`). Flight history and collision samples are bounded and independent of rendering. `src/defend/wisp-arrow.ts` owns each tower’s one/three-arrow inventory, individual 30-hit budgets and shared three-second refill clock; exhausted arrows leave a short deterministic blue spark shower. `src/defend/wisp-fire.ts` adds a separate two-arrow/six-second fire inventory at rank II, timed swept ribbons (two seconds at each point) and a fire-element blast after ten direct hits. Overlapping segments from one fire arrow share one DPS rate; separate arrows stack. Fire arrows are 25% larger than blue arrows. Rank III adds one purple arrow at 175% blue scale: `src/defend/wisp-lightning.ts` locks an in-range aim and commits to a straight piercing ray to the map boundary, regenerating nine seconds after exit. Each point leaves lightning for five seconds at 3× captured attack DPS, including fliers and enemies outside tower range. `src/defend/wisp-trail.ts` shares timed contact-interval unions between fire and lightning. `wisp-core-art.ts` shares scaled silhouettes; elemental tails, ribbons and explosions use procedural art with restrained Reduce Motion variants. [Triple arrows](docs/upgrades/WISP_TRIPLE_ARROWS.md), [Fire arrows](docs/upgrades/WISP_FIRE_ARROWS.md), [Lightning arrow](docs/upgrades/WISP_LIGHTNING_ARROW.md).

The Archer Barracks has three attunements: Skirmishers fire 1/3/5/7 arrows with faster draws and mobile shooting; Javelin Bowmen fire slow massive arrows that carry a file of enemies, gaining unlimited piercing and growing explosions; Skyranger fires anywhere on the battlefield with vertical launch/descent and 10/7/5-shot arrow rain. Existing Ranger purchases become Javelin Bowmen at the same ranks. [Tiers and tuning](docs/upgrades/ARCHER_ATTUNEMENTS.md). `src/defend/archer-attunements.ts` owns the special projectiles; The effects use the existing procedural artwork. `node tests/archer-attunements.mjs` checks desktop/phone combat art and the live three-branch tree.

The Library and Tiles window light uses cached, feathered density fields with screen blending (`src/soft-sunlight.ts`), preserving crisp scene art while sunlight moves slowly through dust. Tiles' pale stained glass and symmetrical leadwork follow the Pixel Art setting. [Window-light reference and tuning](docs/art/WINDOW_SUNLIGHT.md).

Melee troops wear path-colored flowing capes at 60% of the Hero's length when unattuned and 70/80/90% at tiers I/II/III (`src/defend/melee-cape.ts`, sharing the Hero's cloth simulation). Pixel Art enables them; Simple Troops hides them and Reduce Motion holds them still. [Family coverage and tailoring](docs/art/MELEE_CAPES.md).

Melee bodies and equipment face actual movement while walking, nearby targets in combat, and their last heading while idle (`src/defend/melee-facing.ts`). Upright front/back/profile views preserve the Hero, Valkyrie and melee summons' existing style; swordsmen keep their legacy square body and directional equipment. The procedural views are stored in `melee-body-data.ts`. Turning leaves cloth physics in world space. [Coverage, settings and verification](docs/art/MELEE_FACING.md).

The Library's Study and Defend's building Knowledge trees have procedural gold magic circles: projected 4D tesseracts for city Knowledge, inscribed nested triangles for strike spells, and clipped Voronoi diagrams for infantry and tower buildings. [Their visual layers](docs/art/MAGIC_CIRCLE_PROGRESSION.md) grow with completed Knowledge: more framing rings and fine marks, denser central geometry, satellite sigils, recursive cell patterns, connecting rails and moving lights. Building evolution retains earlier transformations and crowned path ranks; the Revolutionary also counts its shared branches. Path and equipped-spell identity reshape the circle without granting extra visual ranks. Purchases, unlearning and respec morph it over 1.7 seconds; pending research only pulses. The effect preserves the chamber artwork, uses bounded pixel canvases and cached geometry, and becomes static under Reduce Motion. `src/magic-circle/` contains the renderer and its configuration; `node tests/magic-circle.mjs` checks desktop/mobile integration and generates a progression reference sheet against the dev server (set `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE` to an installed Chromium).

While a timed Study project runs, the collection and tree give way to the active upgrade at the magic circle's center. Its name, rank, progress, countdown, pause state and cancellation refund stay visible. Completion or cancellation restores the tree; reopening or reloading the Study returns to the active project. The view reuses the existing circle and main loop (`src/ui/ledger.ts`); `node tests/upgrade-work.mjs` checks its desktop/phone layout and research lifecycle.

The checkbox-gated PNG sprite collection, sprite generators and art-generation guidelines have been removed. Buildings, units, terrain, icons and effects use their established procedural/legacy drawings. Existing UI/theme images and fonts remain. The Pixel Art setting is retained for procedural cape, stained-glass and sampling preferences; it loads no replacement PNGs.

Nature ambience is synthesized locally: rain uses soft noise impacts, wind uses filtered noise, and insects use long loops with irregular pauses. Bird and owl calls are sparse and quiet; no audio downloads are required.

`src/ui/hold-repeat.ts` adds accelerating press-and-hold purchases to Mine hires/building upgrades, Library shelves/hires/lab expansion, Tiles purchases and repeatable Smithy/Study upgrades. Holds start after 450 ms, then speed up from 260 ms between purchases to a minimum of 35 ms. Every purchase keeps its current price, capacity and project checks; release, scrolling, cancellation or leaving the controls stops the hold. `node tests/hold-repeat.mjs` checks mouse/touch behavior and persistence with isolated browser contexts (requires the dev server).

Current upgrade costs, per-rank acquisition estimates, production samples and two-year pacing options are in [docs/UPGRADE_TIMING.md](docs/UPGRADE_TIMING.md). Mine crews favor easier ground, digging uneven tunnels and laying rails with one-pixel ramps. Stone takes three times as long as rock, harder stone around 30% down takes another three times as long, and dense stone around 60% down takes another three times. Miners set temporary lamps at dark working faces and hang lasting ceiling lanterns along established tracks; small 1×2 torches burn out. Light lifetimes and graded cart routes survive saves. `node tests/mine-tunnels.mjs` checks the desktop/mobile art and a simulated mining run without player saves (requires the dev server).

Winter freezes boat wakes into persistent ice. Fire and explosions thaw it into drying water; ice blocks rebuilding and makes ground troops slide and recoil from blasts (`ice-motion.ts`). Ponds also freeze in cold weather. Water keeps pixel edges, reflected scenery, rain rings, and walking/sailing wakes. `tests/defend-ice.test.ts` covers gameplay and `node tests/defend-water.mjs` checks visuals without player saves.

`src/defend/progress.ts` saves the selected battle speed for later runs. Defend controls center the start button or separate wave and keep-health boxes. The wave box shows remaining enemies beside a demon icon; keep damage flashes only the sprite and its health bar appears in the header. Builders stranded outside repaired walls panic and run off the board if no intact home is reachable, then despawn alive. `journal.ts` groups discoveries by zone and difficulty, and `defeat.ts` provides compact charts with explicit close buttons. Settings has a master Developer mode checkbox that reveals dev options; turning it off clears those options. Existing saves with cheats enabled acquire the master switch on. Developer mode also adds Unit tester to the Wave picker: start it, open Enemies, and tap an enemy portrait to summon one or hold to keep summoning. `DefendSim.startUnitTester()` suppresses automatic waves and `summonEnemy()` uses ordinary enemy assembly, with the live enemy cap. Tester runs grant no progression or journal discoveries and are never saved.

`src/defend/areas.ts` defines area identities, wave ranges, climate and colors. `city-layer.ts` uses area floor and cap/face textures, while the renderer crossfades city layers and invalidates ground relief on area changes. `area-enemies.ts` supplies themed rosters, each with at least 15 species (Frozen Vault has 20); Nadir includes every normal wave species. `zone-enemy-art.ts` draws 37 native pixel species across eight zones in battle and journal portraits, including regenerating trolls and spirits, fire-resistant forge creatures, armored shells vulnerable to explosions, and broods that release smaller enemies. `waves.ts` preserves random selection when a themed budget leaves an unspendable remainder. `tests/defend-zone-expansion.test.ts` checks wave variety, combat, affordability, caps and saves; `node tests/defend-zone-expansion.mjs` checks desktop/mobile sprites, portraits and persistent discovery with an isolated fixture.

The current stress-test measurements and reproduction steps are in [docs/PERFORMANCE.md](docs/PERFORMANCE.md).

Mine and Library bank up to 24 hours of idle time and automatically fast-forward their actual simulations with a visible countdown, paying gains only as work advances. Unused time is saved. Library fires consume shelf pixels, emit rising cellular smoke and falling ash, and injure only librarians exposed to heat. Shelvers throw buckets, carry charred books outside and repair damaged shelves while preserving intact books. Night watch improves detection and bucket work at night. `node tests/library-idle.mjs` checks desktop/mobile catch-up and fire pixels against an isolated fixture (requires the dev server).

The tower-and-effects stress scene, frame overlay and before/after rendering measurements are in [docs/DEFEND_FRAME_TIMES.md](docs/DEFEND_FRAME_TIMES.md). `defendFrameTimes(true)` in the browser console shows update, entities, effects, terrain and UI timings. Ground-light masks and scorch sprites are cached without rounding their positions, combat pixels are reused between simulation ticks, and tiny effect sprites are baked in software to avoid GPU readbacks. The night's darkness and glow are summed per light on a small field and drawn up to the board once, rather than drawing every light's pool scaled up, and the trees are shaded only within their own box.
