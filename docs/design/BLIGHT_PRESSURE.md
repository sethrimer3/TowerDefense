# Enemy packing and Blight attack transfer

Ground enemies occupy solid body envelopes. Darklings stop flush against other bodies rather than passing through the front line. Their black area therefore grows with their number. Enemy-to-enemy spacing uses the body fills, not two additional outline widths; the shared crimson contour traces the crowd's exterior. Contact with buildings still uses the existing structure/half-art-pixel contour geometry.

Blight means enemies explicitly tagged `faction: "darkOne"`, including all three Darkling sizes. A living, touching Blight chain can transfer its rear members' ordinary contact hits to a front-line enemy's current target. There is no distance multiplier or extra stack bonus: every ready member contributes its own native damage, spends its own attack cooldown, and retains its own damage bonuses. For example, five connected ordinary Darklings have potential unmodified damage of ten per second even if only one can reach the wall. Fronts acquire targets through the native AI; there is no remote target selection through an empty area.

The rules are:

- Transfer requires uninterrupted face contact through living Blight bodies. Gaps, non-Blight bodies, dead fronts and lost contact break it. Corner-only contact is insufficient.
- Several fronts may reach different targets. A rear member follows the nearest front by contact hops; equal paths use stable source IDs. It contributes only once per cooldown, never once per front or path.
- Frozen/staggered rear members cannot strike. A frozen body may still conduct its neighbors' hits while it remains alive and in contact. Impaled bodies and an active teleport do not provide an attacking front.
- Transfer delivers basic damage through the native building/defender defenses. It does not copy an area attack, projectile, summon or death effect. Guarded defenders retain their protection. Bait's oil/thorns act at the front-line contact; killing that front interrupts the remaining delivery.
- Blight approaches defender body/contour contact before biting. A defender charging into a formation is already in contact and can be attacked there. Other enemies retain their weapon reach.
- Ground walkers, boat hulls and fortress cores block one another. Airborne units, underground moles, attached fortress parts and logical Siege target proxies are not independent ground bodies. Their existing altitude/ability rules remain in place.
- Newborns and displaced/teleported bodies receive a deterministic free placement when necessary. Placement cannot repair an overlap by jumping through a wall. If a trapped or physically full entry cannot place a body within the fixed work budget, that reinforcement waits outside the board, cannot attack/conduct, and retries as space opens. Living/wave accounting retains it; no enemy is discarded.

## Cost and implementation

`src/defend/enemy-crowd.ts` owns one sparse quarter-cell index per simulation. Ground bodies span the buckets their envelopes touch; exact local axis sweeps stop movement at the first body, including large steps. There are no all-enemy pair comparisons or moving, stale center-only collision snapshots. A bucket's physical occupancy stays bounded by the minimum one-art-pixel body. Stationary valid bodies are reserved without repeating collision searches; new/displaced bodies use a shared spiral cursor. The total placement probe budget is at most `max(128, 8 × enemy count)` a phase, with at most 64 probes per body.

Multi-source breadth-first passes prepare queues and resolve hits. Each connected body is visited at most once per pass, rather than walking the whole front chain for every attacker. An unchanged formation reuses its previous queue ownership after revalidating its source set; births, deaths, displacements and lost contact invalidate that shortcut. A valid queued rear member skips redundant steering/target searches, while its native cooldown and abilities still advance. Contact geometry is validated once per source; target/front death or displacement stops delivery immediately. Cached metadata lives outside enemy/save objects and is rebuilt/validated on the fixed simulation clock. No random draws, wall-clock dependence, renderer reads or changes to the native release cap are introduced. The regular release cap remains 5,000; 10,000-body fixtures deliberately exceed it to check scaling.

`unit-bodies.ts` centralizes the existing defender body sizes/colors, re-exported by Simple units for existing callers. `contact.ts` uses those sizes for defender contact and routes building approaches through live crowd collision, including its final snap. Normal steering, contact approaches, boat movement, cube movement, ordinary bullet knockback and ice slides share the ground collision gate. Teleports are reconciled at their destination. Dead bodies stop blocking immediately.

Darkling body rectangles fill as one black path in both graphics settings, preventing antialias seams between flush bodies. Pixel-only rectangle paths retain the fast expanded-union geometry. A reusable coverage scratch canvas closes gaps narrower than the combined contour widths before taking the pixel crowd's perimeter; four shifted alpha intersections run only with the combat-cache repaint and clip to the crowd's bounds. No Canvas blur/image filter, scene readback or per-enemy canvas is used. Larger real openings retain their own silhouette edges.

## Validation

`tests/defend-crowd.test.ts` checks mixed-size flush packing, tunneling, bullet knockback and live bucket updates; exact damage/cooldowns; interrupted chains; status and native defense rules; front-line bait retaliation; a defender inside a formation; enemy iteration order; cached-vs-fresh equivalence through topology/target changes; passive scenarios; bounded trapped-entry placement/reentry; coincident spawns; and a 10,000-member connected transfer with exactly one visit per member. Work counters check scaling without machine-dependent timing assertions.

With Vite running, `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/defend-crowd.mjs` writes `test-results/crowd.png` and `.json`. Its 64-Darkling approach has zero overlaps, no waiting bodies and 128 damage when every cooldown is ready. Both graphics modes check flush-body pixels under a fractional camera and absence of crimson seams in closely packed columns. Browser timings cover 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 bodies; measurements are hardware-dependent, not phone-frame guarantees. Existing contour, aura, contact and render-cache probes also apply.

This intentionally changes battle movement, melee contact, crowd damage opportunities and subsequent outcomes. Defend replay goldens are re-recorded for that reason; city generation, fence and save fixtures stay fixed. Wave compositions and migration checkboxes do not change.

All Dark One enemies now share the fiery noise portal outline. Packed pixel cracks receive soft black infill rather than exposing scenery after interior contour removal. This is presentation only: infill never changes collision, contact or attack transfer. Wide passages stay open, complex sprites do not enter the infill mask, and player body colors remain intact. See [unit contours](../art/UNIT_CONTOURS.md) and `tests/blight-contours.mjs`.
