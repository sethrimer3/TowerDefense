# World pixels and unit visibility contours

The canonical world lattice is **8 art pixels per board cell**, defined in `src/defend/art-scale.ts`. A city tile spans 8 cells, or 64 art pixels. One art pixel is a world-art measure, not a fixed CSS/device pixel: its screen size changes with the renderer's pixels-per-cell, camera zoom and display density. Older artists still round their strokes independently. Parks re-export the canonical constant; shared effects and Darkling bodies use it directly.

The visibility contour extends **half an art pixel outside the body**, or **1/16 of a cell**. Siege opponents retain deep crimson (`#9c1235`), and dark navy (`#121d42`) for player troops, including friendly dark wizards, summons and shades. Every other unit gets the same half-art-pixel contour in the ink its old baked rim had: black (`#140c0a`) for neutral enemies and civilians, gold (`#f2c94c`) while a watch tower marks an enemy and violet (`#6b3d8f`) for the brood. No unit keeps a full-art-pixel rim: procedural painters and source rows omit exterior rim pixels, so the Merged unit outlines setting no longer changes how units look. Garrison badges remain UI badges.

All `darkOne` enemies instead share the **fiery noise portal contour**, with widths of **0.5 art pixel for Mini Darklings**, **0.75 for Darklings**, and **1 for larger bodies**, including Major/Significant, the Blight Worm and complex Dark One sprites. The outward width is measured in world art pixels, scaling with camera/DPR. Tiny widths are captured per rectangle (and artist sprite body) within the same faction union, without extra layers or separate body fills. The same screen-anchored scrolling ridged-noise texture paints one channel for the whole faction; no static crimson rim remains on Blight. `portalOutline` can opt another faction into this treatment. Player and neutral contour widths/colors stay unchanged. Reduce Motion freezes the noise; Effects Off retains the visibility cue. Noise patterns are cached per context so multiple renderers/previews do not regenerate the tile while alternating.

The contour follows the union of each group's filled body shapes. Touching and overlapping bodies have a single perimeter; no contour crosses another unit body, even across opposing groups or translucent cloaks. At small phone scales a half-art-pixel contour has partial screen-pixel coverage. Rounding it up to a full screen pixel would make it too thick, so the renderer preserves the fractional coverage.

`UnitOutline` retains the legacy painter for compatibility checks and adds a thin mode backed by `UnitContours`. Primitive fills and strokes contribute vector geometry; baked sprite bodies contribute cached paths from their opaque row runs without color-based stripping. Crop paths are intersected with source bounds and cached with a fixed limit. Live capes invalidate their geometry with their existing sprite copies. Axis-aligned pixel bodies use a batched union of expanded rectangles, avoiding the stroker's extra geometry for crowds. Other filled paths are stroked at twice the desired outward width; then all body interiors are removed. The remaining outside half is the visible contour. Cloaked units/shades lower the capture threshold for their bodies while ordinary sprites still exclude faint auras.

The normal renderer always enables these merged visibility contours, including when the older Merged outlines setting is off. That setting continues to control ordinary neutral/black rims. Buffers rebuild only when the existing combat cache repaints, including pan/zoom, size and settings changes. A screen-space overlay reuses the cached contours after world color drain, forest canopies and smoke; this keeps allegiance colors visible through the wash and cover. It draws before targeting/editing markers and HUD controls. Bodies retain their own palettes and normal world effects.

`dark-drain.ts` shares its bucket counts with a second, smaller-radius night heat field. Dark One enemies have a faint deep neon orange halo alone; local groups increase its strength and radius. The shared world clock enables it only in the actual night phase; day, sunset, dusk, twilight and dawn have none. Legacy/night-only render callers still fade it with their night intensity. The tint is `#d43a06` at maximum opacity 0.32, preserving a dim appearance even in large groups. Both fields have smooth radial falloff and bounded max blending; there are no individual enemy lights or blur filters. The drain now starts visibly at roughly 22% at ten neighbors, then eases up to full grayscale near sixty while preserving tonal contrast. The former 30% source-over gray wash is removed; the saturation blend preserves luminosity and the original black-to-white range. The old ~2% onset was too faint against the forest.

The surrounding scenery is washed after terrain, lighting, ground effects, water, trees, smoke, floating structures and rain have been painted. While the aura is active, combat art retains its own reusable screen layer; its alpha is subtracted from the wash/glow mask. This protects unit bodies and projectiles as well as their later contour overlay, without repainting them over canopies or reading scene pixels. Fractional sprite edges retain their alpha coverage. Both fields rasterize at most 10 Hz while battle time advances; the actor layer shares combat-cache invalidation, including camera, size and graphics changes. Effects Off and Reduce Motion retain these steady cues. These presentation changes do not alter battle state or replay fixtures.

There is no per-frame scene readback, per-unit canvas, image filter or pairwise overlap search. Sprite paths share the existing once-per-sprite readback, and each ink uses one reusable screen-sized contour buffer. Crowd work scales with painted geometry, not the number of overlaps.

Contact damage uses `defend/contact.ts`, entirely in world space. A joined stone wall keeps its existing one-art-pixel outline inside its painted box. The enemy's outward half-art-pixel rim touches that box, leaving one art pixel between the two body fills, completely occupied by the two rims. The shared `WALL_FACE_HANG` also accounts for the wall's south-facing masonry below its collision cell. Darkling bodies retain exact fractional art size and positions in both graphics modes; rounding them independently would reintroduce a zoom-dependent gap.

The old enemy-half-size + 0.4-cell allowance still identifies nearby targets, but damage waits until `approachBuildingContact` reaches the perimeter. The final step clamps to contact, bypassing the ordinary movement dead zone and crowd separation only during this approach. Mini Darklings use a suitably smaller approach collider; wooden rails allow entry into their own empty cell margin while other owners still block movement. Other buildings retain their native contact footprints. Their shared one-art-pixel outside sprite ring is presentation padding; it does not move collision geometry. Native/scenario routes, boat rams and cardinal ice-cube strikes share this gate; ranged attacks and unit-versus-unit weapon reach retain their own rules. The earlier contact migration changed attack timing and re-recorded battle replays. The outline-source migration changes no contact code, simulation state or goldens.

Validation:

- Contact geometry for all three Darkling sizes, wall faces/corners, thin rails, zero-time/frozen movement, native routes and specialist attackers; 54 real-renderer contact frames across scales/zooms and both graphics modes.
- Native canvas pixel samples at 0.5, 1 and 2 screen-pixel outward widths; black body preservation and no overlap seams.
- Differently colored opposing units, translucent overlaps, outline-free sources, source cropping and camera transforms.
- Production rendering under 16 desktop/phone, Simple-unit, Merged outlines and Reduce Motion combinations, including maximum grayscale drain. Phone coverage is compared with a frame without contours rather than requiring fully opaque color samples.
- Existing troop-family gallery, both sides and Simple/sprite art, on grass and at night.
- A 5,000-square crowd checks fixed canvas count and zero frame readbacks; timings are recorded in ignored test-results and remain hardware-dependent.
- Combat-cache reuse, invalidation and fresh-paint pixels; legacy painter compatibility remains independently checked. The contour-only checks do not change gameplay fixtures.

Run the Vite server, then `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/unit-contours.mjs`, `node tests/siege-outlines.mjs` and `npm run test:render-cache` (set the same browser variable as needed). Artifacts are written to `test-results/`.

Contact checks: `node --experimental-transform-types --import ./tests/pin-random.ts --test tests/defend-contact.test.ts`, then `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/defend-contact.mjs` with Vite running.

`PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/darkling-aura.mjs` checks visible onset, local group heat, night fade/day removal, unchanged opaque unit/shot pixels in the full renderer, trees/terrain/building desaturation, pause stability, desktop/phone DPR, and 10,000-enemy allocation/readback bounds. It writes a day/night comparison and measurements to `test-results/darkling-aura.*`.

Aura blends are clipped to the fields’ affected world bounds (including interpolation margins); a small crowd does not require a full-viewport blend. Bounds and screen protection follow the same camera transform.

Darkling formations fill their black rectangles as one path in both graphics modes, avoiding fractional-camera antialias seams between touching bodies. Rectangle-only paths retain expanded-union contour geometry. `mergePixelGaps` explicitly captures only pixel body rectangles. A soft morphological closing fills narrow cracks **black** instead of leaving bare scenery where interior rims were erased. Three nested expansion bands feather the coverage; center plus four diagonal intersections keep wide openings clear and gradually darken necks as bodies approach. Actual body interiors are subtracted from contours before the shared black mask is inserted behind them, preserving allied/sprite colors. The portal mixes that mask into opaque fire, so partially joined necks show black/fire instead of leaking scenery. Torch shine excludes the black joins. Infill lives in the actor layer, so scenery drain cannot wash it out. It reverses from current positions with no temporal trails. One scratch canvas is reused and clipped to the pixel crowd bounds; complex sprites sharing the portal ink do not enter the infill mask. Native mask insertion bypasses sprite caching/readback. This runs with the combat cache, without Canvas filters or scene reads. Large real openings retain silhouette edges. `tests/defend-crowd.mjs` checks flush and narrowly separated columns in normal/Simple art. Physical body spacing and attack-transfer rules are in [Blight pressure](../design/BLIGHT_PRESSURE.md).

Buildings and other world art use `damage-art.ts`'s shared one-art-pixel outside
ring. Unit source canvases explicitly opt out and preserve their original
logical boxes; `UnitContours` is their only outline. Dark material pixels are
never stripped. All source frames are exported and replaceable through
`art/sprites/`; see that directory's README. `tests/defend-outline-sprites.mjs`
checks source placement, separate body/rim lighting masks, joined edges and
Crisp on/off screenshots at desktop and phone sizes.

`PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/blight-contours.mjs` checks every Dark One species in normal/Simple modes, solid black tight cracks, partial neck coverage, reopening/wide gaps, preserved player bodies, complex-sprite exclusion, and 1,000/5,000/10,000-enemy geometry+noise draws with zero warm canvas allocation/readbacks. Timings are hardware-dependent; the stress size is not a frame-rate guarantee.

The aura checks also compare every shared-clock time-of-day phase against a frame without the aura: only night changes with a lone Blight. `tests/defend-darklings.mjs` retains the 20–220 gray test span unchanged, verifying that grayscale no longer reduces contrast.
