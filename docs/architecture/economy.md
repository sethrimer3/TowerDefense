# Mine, Library and economy

The idle Mine and Library, metals, Knowledge, timed upgrade work, Realm workshops, economy tiles and the shared play-time sky.

Moved from `AGENTS.md`; keep it current with the code it describes.

**Mine** is the idle side of the game, in `src/mine/`, saved as `save.mine` (null until it first runs). `world.ts` holds the grid (384 × 480 cells, one material each), the world a seed generates (`generate`, `strata`: sky, grass, dirt with rocks, stone with copper, deeper silver and deeper still gold veins, bedrock) and the falling-sand `World.step`: dirt, grass and spoil fall and slide only onto drops of two or more, so slopes settle one across and two up; rocks drop straight; only awake 16 × 16 chunks are stepped. Gravel and loose dirt (pockets in the stone and dirt) slide on any drop and, like falling rock, crush the ladders and timber they land on; lava pools deep down creep slowly and turn to stone where water meets them. `caves` carves natural caves into the stone after the veins (winding passages and wide caverns, hung with stalactites and grown with stalagmites, kept clear of the shaft and the dirt), and lava keeps away from them. A separate `water` layer (one flag a cell) flows down, spreads level and sinks under falling ground. `buildings.ts` lays out the surface buildings either side of the shaft at their levels (`layout(surface, shaftX, levels)`, 1 to `MAX_LEVEL` each, wider each level: the shaft house, the barracks with a lounge and a bay of bunks a level, the warehouse, the forge with two hands' spots a level and the smithy with an anvil a level, `anvils`), each with spots (bunks, stools, stations) and the scripted path to each from the door (`along`, `pathTicks`): a miner inside a building is out of the grid, its feet left at the door. `sim.ts` (`MineSim`) is the operation: a plan grid marks cells to dig or fit (ladder, rail, torch, lamp, `P_BEAM`; `PLAN_MARKS` bounds the decoded plan); the planner sinks the laddered shaft level by level and drives a tunnel out both sides of each (dug plain; with the Mine carts skill, `cartTrack`, `layTrack` lays rail only on every `TRACK_EVERY`th level and only `TRACK_REACH` cells out each side, crossing caves on a trestle of beams), with now and then a drift slanting up or down off it behind a few rungs of ladder (`planDrift`: level or slanting, lit, ending in a chamber, stopping short of soil, boulders, loose ground and lava) and, from the second level, a winze laddered down from the level above; a dig that breaks into an unworked cave (`discover`) floods its extent into the plan and plans its work (`planCave`: torches along its floors, the ore on its walls by `markVein`, drifts from its ends and lowest point); without Ore sense a dig only plans ore within a few cells of it, while with the Ore sense skill (`oreSense`) the planner also cuts ways to unplanned ore within `SENSE_REACH` (15 cells, 30 art pixels) of the workings: `sensedOre` queues it by distance and `senseNear` picks after each dig, `routeToOre` tries `orePath`'s stairs, ramps, ladder-first and level-first routes through `carve` (two tall, climbs dug plain and laddered as the crew climb, torches on level runs, nothing near the shaft, lava, bedrock or loose ground) and marks the vein; it goes no deeper than `senseFloor` (the deepest level's row plus 2), takes only veins worth the cut (`VEIN_WORTH`, distance over vein size) until the shaft is at its last level, then every lump; route cells (`side`, unsaved) wait `SIDE_WAIT` behind the trunk's work and dig in `SEAM_CUT` of the time, and `stepSense` works the queue every 15 ticks; each miner (1 × 2 cells) picks the nearest unclaimed job by a breadth-first search over where it can stand (walk, step up two, climb or put up ladders underground, drop), shores loose ground beside its digs with timber, follows ore it bares, carries ore to the yard by the shaft house (and spoil to the heap), tips it at the shaft's foot on its level (`shaftFoot`, `loadBucket`) or into a level's cart where track was laid, which rolls to the shaft, where its load waits (`buckets`) for the hoist: one bucket (`hoist`) let down to each load in turn and wound up to tip it in the `yard`. Forge hands carry the yard's ore into the forge (`fetch`, `CARRY` at a time, gold first) while it holds less than `FORGE_CAP`, so a short-handed forge leaves the backlog in the yard. Each miner has a name for life (`names.ts`, `minerName`: hashed from the seed and the hire, unlike the rest of the crew's) and a trade (`job`: mine, forge, smith) the player sets per miner in the crew list (`setJob`; `setJobs` puts counts to the trades, `jobs` counts them; the second hire goes to the forge and the fifth to the smithy while they have nobody): forge hands smelt the ore piles (`ore`, a `Metals` record of copper, silver and gold, as are packs, carts, the yard and the hoist) into bars (`bars`, `ORE_PER_BAR` ore each) and smiths work the bars, every `BARS_PER_POINT` of a metal making a Smithy point of it, each at a station inside (`stepInside`, `craft`; a trade with nobody works at a quarter pace); fittings and shoring use supplies (`kit`) fetched from the warehouse; at night (`night`) all but the night shift (`nightShift`: 20%, plus 5% a rank of the Coffee skill; the Mine tree's one-rank Mine carts (`mineCarts`) and Ore sense (`oreSense`) skills reach the sim through the host's `upgrades()`, which `MinePage.advance` sets as `cartTrack` and `oreSense` every frame, carried to the next prospect) sleep in their bunks; hungry miners eat in the lounge; the shaft house keeps a share of the runoff out of the shaft (`seal`: 20%, plus 15% a rank of Waterproofing) and its roof, like every building's, takes the lightning. The sky turns through day and night (`daylight`, one day every `DAY_TICKS`) and spells of `weatherOf` the seed (clear, cloudy, rain, storm; `skyAt` eases between them): rain falls as water that soaks into soil, runs down the shaft and floods the workings, where miners bail it (and douse fires) as jobs; in storms lightning strikes (and can set wood alight) and miners at the surface sit it out in the lounge. Hazards (`hazards`, `struckBy`, `survey`, `stepFire`) can kill a miner by crushing, drowning, starving trapped (`fed`, `MEAL_TICKS`), lava, fire or lightning; `kill` spares the last miner, any death within `DEATH_GAP` of the last (a new mine starts with that grace) and a second death in one catch-up, records the cause in `lost` and the miner in `fallen` (until the player taps it away, `dismiss`, or hires again), and posts `news` the page and renderer show. A prospect's ore is finite (`oreFound`, `oreLeft` as of the last `survey`): once the shaft is at the deepest level and the planned work is done (or has stopped shrinking for five minutes) it is `workedOut`, and `prospectNext(seed)` builds the next prospect, a fresh world, carrying the crew, the stock and the losses (a `MineSave` without `cells` is such a prospect: the crew starts at the barracks). Each building has a level (`buildingLevels`, raised with metal by `upgrade(id)` at `upgradeCost`, refused by `upgradeBlock`): the barracks bunks `CREW_PER_LEVEL` a level (`crewCap`, which `hire` keeps to), the forge has room for `FORGE_PER_LEVEL` hands and the smithy for `SMITHS_PER_LEVEL` smiths (`jobCap`, which `setJob`/`setJobs` keep to; the forge also holds more ore, `forgeCap`), the shaft house keeps out more rain, and the warehouse holds `STOCK_PER_LEVEL` supplies and brings in `STOCK_RATE` a minute a level (`stock`, `stockCap`, `stockRate`) and lets the others rise one level past it (`maxLevel`). Miners fetch their kit from `stock`, so with none the fittings (and the shaft) wait. An upgrade rebuilds the building and moves any it pushes along (`rebuilds`: taken down at `from`, then raised; shut meanwhile, everyone inside sent out, its trade's hands working the face), using supplies as it goes and waiting when there are none; `setLevel` sets a level at once (tests, the console). Fewer miners make the next hire cheaper (`sim.price`). `collect()` hands over the Smithy points made (into `save.smithy`); `hirePrice` prices miners in metal. Sinking the shaft and the ladders a climbing miner puts up use supplies too, so with none the shaft waits. Smiths working a Smithy upgrade can't be dragged out of the smithy (the host's `busySmiths`); Master smith makes room for another (`extraSmiths`). The mine uses no shared random stream: generation and choices hash the seed, cells and tick. `art.ts` paints the surface structures as pixel art at two pixels a cell (`paintWall`, `paintBack`, `paintShell`: clapboards, board and batten or ashlar with windows, back walls, posts, doors, tiled, slated or shingled roofs and the forge's brick chimney; `paintHeadframe`, `paintGrave`), each baked once into a canvas by `Sprites` with a copy per step of light. `figures.ts` draws the miners as 2 × 4 half-cell figures (`drawFigure` in a `Pose`: standing, walking, climbing with arms reaching, at work with an arm out, reaching overhead, sitting, crouched; `drawSleeper`, breathing) in an `outfit` picked from each one's name within its trade; `acts.ts` times the crew's movements on the wall clock, presentation only: each miner's `temperament` (two favourite habits from its name), the habit a waiting miner is in (`habitAt`, `habitPose`: looking about, stretching, sitting, whistling, yawning, tapping a foot, sifting the ground), a forge hand's shovel cycle (`shovelAt`) and a smith's strokes on a cooling bar until it is quenched (`hammerAt`); the renderer adds chats between miners waiting side by side, pick and hammer swings, buckets dipped, sweat when hungry, hat lamps' glow, the furnace flaring at each throw, the bellows, the winch drum and the headframe wheel's spokes turning with the hoist; `ore-sparkle.ts` (presentation only) makes ore near the lit workings glint once Ore sense is learnt, and the ore heaped in the yard and the forge always (`heapGlint`, `drawGlint`): `sparkleSites` (a chamfer distance from passages below the surface lit past `SPARKLE.lit`, found again when the world changes, at most once a second, while effects are on) keeps a hashed `share` of the copper, silver and gold cells within `SPARKLE.reach` (30) art pixels, and `sparkleFrame` times each one's `FRAMES` (a dot growing to a star in its ore's `GLINT` colours and back) from its cell and the wall clock, drawn by the renderer's `drawSparkles`, fainter further from the light (`tests/mine-sparkle.test.ts`); `particles.ts` (`Particles`) moves the effects each its own way (dust and grit fall and settle, chimney smoke and steam rise and drift, sparks arc, z's, wisps and whistled notes float up, embers drift up flickering, drops fall, talk shows as dots). `render.ts` (`MineRenderer`) paints visible rows two pixels a cell each way into an offscreen image (the miners' pixel size), each cell's four pixels given its material's detail (grains, specks, cracks, ore set in stone, grass blades, rungs, rails and sleepers, flames) and a lit lip or shadow where it meets open air, lit by a two-channel light map (daylight, and torches, lamps, lava, fires and miners) spread cell to cell and blended between cells per pixel (unlit cells take a fast path; the daylight is spread from full day and the moment's light taken off it per pixel, `skyAt`, so day and night turn smoothly every frame, repainting as the sky changes, however fast the mine runs), the back wall's lumps (`bump`) bevelled toward the warm light and solid edges facing it given a thin warm sheen, torches and hanging lanterns drawn each frame flickering in their own time (`flickerAt`), each lantern's bulb 5 or 6 pixels over the floor below it (`lanternBulb`; one hung before that rule keeps its old place), and repainted for the world's changes at most every 60 ms, under a pan/zoom camera (an even number of device pixels a cell, so each pixel is whole and they meet without seams; zoom 1 shows `VIEW_CELLS` across the view, in to `MAX_ZOOM`; `target` follows one miner) with stars by night, clouds, rain and lightning (pixel bolts with forks, the flash dimly lighting only the sky above the ground line), free ladders in caves drawn as braced scaffolding and timber over open ground as a trestle, the headframe, the buildings (each front wall fading while anyone is inside, to show the bunks, lounge, shelves, furnace and ore piles, anvil and bars, and the miners sleeping or at work; the shaft house's lantern and the barracks' windows lit at night), graves for lost miners, carts, the hoist's rope and bucket (eased between steps), the yard's ore, the forge's heap and shelves filling toward `FORGE_CAP`, the miners and the effects over it (from `stream("effects")`), with half cells snapped to whole screen pixels (`fine`); a building tapped (`picked`, `buildingAt`) is outlined and shown open. `ui.ts` (`MinePage`) is the tab: drag pans, the wheel and a pinch zoom; a tapped building shows its stats, level and upgrade button (or how its rebuild goes); the Crew button slides the crew list in beside the view (names dragged between the trades' boxes, each showing its room, tapped to follow; the renderer's `refWidth` keeps the zoom while the view narrows); a tap on a building shows its stats over the view (bunks, ore in the forge, bars waiting, the hoist); New Prospect shows only once the crew may move on (`canMoveOn`: under `MOVE_ON_SHARE`, a fifth, of the prospect's ore left, or worked out) and says so before moving on; a tap on its fast-forwarding note fades it away (`fadeAway`) until the catching up is done; and `advance(now)` spends the banked time tick by tick within a 6 ms frame budget and a maximum speed of 120 times normal. Up to 24 hours of remaining time are saved as `idleMs`; every gain comes from simulated work, with no historical-pace payout. `pace` remains a saved measurement of recent smithy output. `away` (`MineAway`) keeps the growing points and losses for the welcome-back screen. `tests/mine.test.ts` covers the physics (water, gravel, lava, fire), generation, the crew's work and pay, the buildings, trades, night shift, supplies and waterproofing, weather, floods, deaths and their spacing, names and the lost, prospects, the hoist and the yard, building levels, caps, supplies and rebuilds, and the save; `tests/mine-particles.test.ts` how each effect moves; `tests/mine-acts.test.ts` the temperaments, habits, shovel and hammer cycles.

**Mine tunnels and lighting.** Stone takes 330 ticks (three times rock); `HARD_STONE` at about 30% underground depth takes 990, and `DENSE_STONE` at about 60% takes 2970. Softer seams cross the deeper strata, and ore/caves work in all three. `routeTunnel` minimizes excavation cost within three rows of a level, with seeded bends and at most a one-cell grade; roof pockets vary the clearance. `tunnelRows` drives rail reach, progression and carts, while the renderer connects rails in one-art-pixel increments. Digging beneath rails plans replacement trestles. Miners set `WORK_LAMP` beside dark working faces, replace spent lamps during long jobs, and plan/build lasting `LAMP` lanterns along established track with irregular gaps, only where the open height from rock to rock (track, water and fittings count as open) is at least `LANTERN_ROOM` cells (8 pixels), so ordinary 3-cell tunnels get them only in roof pockets, caves and chambers; the bulb hangs 5 or 6 pixels over the floor, and each level's shaft-foot lantern hangs in a pocket dug over the tunnel's mouth. Work lamps last 90 simulated seconds; torches last a seeded 120-240 seconds, and spent torch plans become plain dig marks. Lighting checks flood through open cells, so a lamp behind a wall does not suppress a work light. Torches are the right two pixels of a cell (1x2 art pixels); lanterns have a one-pixel-wide ceiling chain and a bottom flame. Carts are three art-pixel rows tall, including their load, with no visible wheels; metal and ore sample the cave's local daylight and warm lighting per pixel. Saves retain remaining light lifetimes (`lights`), compressed floor offsets (`routes`), cart elevations and `geology: 1`; older routes are reconstructed from rail plans, and older prospects upgrade only untouched deep stone, preserving their excavated passages, ore and fittings. Run `tests/mine-tunnels.test.ts` and `node tests/mine-tunnels.mjs` (dev server required; desktop/mobile galleries in `test-results/`, no player-save access).

**Ore sparkles.** `mine/ore-sparkle.ts` draws a single ore-colored art pixel with a smooth opacity fade over 550 ms, every 52–120 seconds (1/20 of the former frequency), for both Ore sense and surface heaps. No simulation RNG or save changes.

**Mine sun.** `mine/render.ts` paints the shared sun after sky/stars, at the native half-cell pixel resolution with a lattice-snapped centre. Its circular disc keeps one silhouette during solar motion and camera changes; the opaque disc covers stars and the translucent halo warms the sky behind it. Terrain, water and foreground layers still occlude it. `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/mine-sun.mjs` checks full-disc invariance/symmetry through hundreds of solar positions and zooms, real stars behind its disc, horizon disappearance and native desktop/phone screenshots. No simulation/save/RNG changes.

**Library** is a cathedral nave the player fills, in `src/library/`, saved as `save.library` (null until it first shows); it earns Knowledge, `knowledgeRate` = built shelves × professors an hour. `geometry.ts` holds where things stand, in pixels (192 × 300, the floor's top at `FLOOR`; a hallway `HALL` long out of each side wall, with `EXITS` at their ends and a water butt in each, `BUTTS`; the return shelf, `RETURN`, holding `RETURN_BOOKS`; and below the nave the alchemy lab, `LAB_TOP` to `LAB_FLOOR`, the picture `WORLD_H` tall, its ladder down from a trapdoor at `STAIR_X` and its stations in `LAB`). `sim.ts` (`LibrarySim`): each shelf unit (two rows of seven books) has a plank count in `units` (-1 none, 0 an outline once bought, `PLANKS` built); units stack in ten bays against the back wall in `SHELF_ORDER` up to `SHELF_TOP`, under the stained-glass `WINDOW`. Librarians (2 × 4 pixels, each with a hat and robe of their role) have a name for life (`librarianName`) and a `role` (`ROLES`): a hire takes `roleFor`'s pick (the first a professor, the second a shelver, the third a researcher, then whichever of shelving and reading has fewer) and the player changes it in the tab's Staff list (`setRole`, which drops what they were doing). Shelvers (and professors while there are no shelvers, `shelving`) do the stacks' work below; professors (`readJob`) take a fresh book down, read it at a table seat (`study`) or standing, and put it, read (`spent`, counted in `read`), on the return shelf (`returns`, `returnJob`); every book on the shelves is unread. Researchers climb down to the lab, where `lab.ts` (`labErrand`) gives them errands station to station (an ingredient from the jars poured into the cauldron and stirred, the athanor's bellows, the alembic, whose full receiver feeds the philosopher's stone, the desk, the mortar, a chant at the circle, the homunculus) over the apparatus's state (`LabState`, `sim.lab`: furnace, brew, still, stone, glow, puffs, busy stations; `stepLab`), now and then a brew going up in smoke and leaving its brewer sooty (`soot`) and a `mess` someone sweeps up, tastings (floating, hiccups), scrolls, feeding the homunculus and conferring at the desk; `researched()` flares the circle when a research is bought. The lab has a level (`sim.labLevel`, 1 to `LAB_MAX_LEVEL`, raised with metal at `labPrice` by `upgradeLab`) and room for one researcher a level (`researcherCap`, kept by `hire`, `setRole` and `roleFor`'s `room`); levels open `ANNEXES` west and east under the hallways, whose stations (`LAB_NEEDS`, `hasStation`) add errands: the mandrake garden (a pulled one shrieks, `shriek`), the lectern, the crucible casting gold (`cast`, `casts`), the orrery (`orrery`), the salamander (`breath`) and the orb (`orb`). They follow step lists (walk, climb, timed work, `away` beyond a hallway's end): one fetches the wheelbarrow of planks (`barrow`) when building wants planks, and the rest carry planks from it to ladders (one segment a plank, up to the built shelves), outlines and burnt tables; another wheels in the book cart (`bookCart`) of new books when shelves stand empty, which the rest shelve; when the return shelf is getting full (or the cart goes for new books anyway) one loads its books into the cart (`exportJob`) and wheels them out, bringing back fresh ones for the gaps (`booksIn`/`booksOut`); otherwise they sort the stacks (each colour has its bay, `homeBay`, and books are shelved there where there is room; `sorted`) or tidy books between bays. Each librarian has `energy`, drained by work (`TIRING`) and restored by a cup of tea fetched from outside or, at night, a doze in a free seat; by night (`night`, which the page sets from `daylight`) about half the shelvers and professors go home down a hallway (`home`) and return by day, researchers working on; now and then two stop to talk (`with`) or one gazes up at the window. As dusk falls (`lanternClock`: from `LANTERN_DUSK` to `LANTERN_DAWN` of the shown sky's day, which the page passes as `toDawn`, seconds left) shelvers fetch 2 to 4 lit `Lantern`s in from the hallways and carry them up the ladders onto the tops of different bays (`fetchLantern`, `lanternJob`), each candle with an even chance of going out (`outAt`) 1 to 60 seconds before dawn; by day they take them down and carry them out (`lowerLantern`); placed ones are saved (`lanterns`, optional). The renderer draws them outline-free and lights the nave from each lit one (`drawLanterns`, `lanternAt`); `tests/library-lanterns.test.ts`. After a death the others mourn at the ashes (`remains`), then one sweeps them up and scrubs low soot off the stone (`Fire.scrub`). The two water butts hold `BUTT_FULL` buckets: fire fighters draw from them (and run out to the well when both are dry), those who fled refill them during a fire, and one refills them afterwards. Every book is in a slot, a hand, on a table or in the cart. With the Enchanted ink skill (`enchant`, its ranks), each shelved book has `enchantChance` (1 in `ENCHANT_ODDS`, 10,000, a rank) a minute of becoming enchanted (`sim.enchanted`, a flag per slot that travels with the book in hand, `rune`); a professor reading one gets `enchantedGift` (one minute of the base rate, at least 1) Knowledge at once (`takeBonus`, which the page pays; `runesRead` counts it), and the renderer draws runes glowing up its spine (`drawRunes`); zoomed in, each rune pixel cross-fades (`glyphShare`, by screen pixels to a Library pixel) into a small glyph in its colour drawn in pixels a fifth the size (`glyphs.ts`: `GLYPHS`, 7 × 7 fine pixels, `glyphFor` steady per book, `glowOf` a faint glow round the strokes; `tests/library-glyphs.test.ts`), and about three in four other shelved books show a title down the spine (`drawTitles`: two or three 5 × 5 runic letters, `TITLE_GLYPHS`, `titleFor`, very dark brown on light covers and dark gold on dark ones, `titleInk`, lit by the nave's light, each letter a cached sprite in its lit colour, only books in view and where the spine shows); only books with neither title nor runes may wear a deep gold band across the spine (`BAND_GOLD`; `slotsKey` counts enchanted books so the shelves repaint). Once a minute a whole table catches fire with `accidentChance(fireproof)` (1% × 0.9 a rank of Fireproof Wood); `fire.ts` (`Fire`) simulates it on a 2-pixel grid of heat and fuel (rising heat, wet cells, thrown droplets that douse with `fireDrill(fireTraining).douse`). Librarians in the nave notice it after a moment, then flee down a hallway or (all shelvers, plus a trained share of professors shelving) fetch buckets from the butts and throw them; heat kills (`deaths`, `remains`); the lab is out of its reach, and the return shelf burns with its books. Fuel consumption removes individual artwork pixels. Smoke rises through empty cells and ash falls and piles up on the floor. When it is out, `settleFire` retains every purchased shelf and intact book, queues missing planks for repair in place, and leaves charred books for shelvers to carry outside, one a trip, after building and fetching planks so rebuilding never waits on them (`burntSlots`, `burntLoose`, `burntFrom`; a charred book whose shelf has burnt away under it falls to the floor, `dropBurnt`, rather than hang in the air); `lost` says what it cost. Night watch speeds detection and bucket work by 15% per rank during simulated nights. It steps on every tab (the page's `advance`, not while the game is closed) and draws from its own `random(seed)`; `furnish(n)` builds and stocks shelves at once (tests, `libraryDebug`). `render.ts` (`LibraryRenderer`) bakes the nave once (ashlar walls and piers with a height map, from `ashlar.ts` (`ashlar`, `facing`), the stone the hallways and the lab share, the piers down to the floor, outlined by `pillarShade` and shaded round after the light by `roundness`, so a torch on a pier can't wash it flat (their feet, `PIER_FRONT` up, drawn again over the librarians, so those going to and from the hallways pass behind them), the vault, the floor, the lancet window with its rose in an oak frame and sill, the hallways) and lights it per pixel each frame: ambient and window light by `daylight(now)` (one day and night every `DAY_MS` of wall-clock time) plus the torches, candles and the fire's blazes through the blocks' slopes (flames, here and in the lab, flicker on the wall clock, `flame`, so they stay calm while idle time fast-forwards); it paints outlines, half-built shelves, char and soot, and draws carts, librarians with what they hold, flames, embers and water over it, and the fire's smoke and ash a pixel at a time into one lit image (`drawSoot`: the ash mottled in clumps with pale flecks and charcoal, its crust lighter and the heap darker deeper down, embers in it while hot; thick smoke opaque, only its thin edges letting the wall show through), so cells meet without seams; the hallways (ashlar walls, beamed ceiling, the nave's own flagstones (`flag`), bump mapped) show only where librarians have lately walked, lit per pixel by whoever walks them, each one's candle coming up over the first `CANDLE_RAMP` pixels out of the nave (`candleUp`) and its light coming in through the door onto the piers and nearest shelves (`DOOR_GLOW`), less the further out they are (`DOOR_REACH`); smoke and ash are lit by the light without the stones' bevels (`flat`), so the bevels never show through them; the sunbeams and their dust stay inside the nave (`inNave`), a mote that drifts out gone at once; the ladder down is hidden under the nave's floor (only its top shows at the trapdoor's slot) and librarians on it are clipped out between the floor and the lab's vault (`HATCH_Y`, under the vault's rib: the ladder and the athanor's chimney stand behind the rib, `behindRib`); the return shelf and the trapdoor are painted into the nave, and `lab-render.ts` (`LabRenderer`) bakes the lab once a level (earth with roots, masonry, the nave's ashlar on the back wall and pilasters under three barrel vaults, bump mapped like the nave's, the open annexes with their own vaults and furnishings, flagged floor, chalked signs and the transmutation circle, the jars, alembic, athanor (its bricks bump mapped, `bump`) and bellows, pedestal, hanging crocodile, cauldron, desk, mortar bench, the homunculus's jar, lanterns) and lights it per pixel by its fires, candles, lanterns, glowing runes, stone and circle, through the stones' slopes, with flames, bubbles, steam, drips, embers, a spider (out of sight behind the vault's rib but in about a third of two-minute spells, `SPIDER`), the black cat (presentation only) and the annexes' marvels drawn over it, and glow sprites, sparks and coloured smoke over the researchers (`drawOver`), only while it is in view; its animations (and the cat, sparks, smoke clouds and the moments `since` times: shrieks, breaths, castings) run on the wall clock, so fast-forwarding through idle time doesn't race them. The camera pans down to `WORLD_H` (drag), zooms smoothly about the pointer from the whole nave in to `MAX_ZOOM` (`zoomBy`: the wheel, a pinch or a double-tap, as in the Mine), and can follow a librarian (`target`) or glide between nave and lab (`goal`). `ui.ts` (`LibraryPage`) is the tab, with its tally (one stat a line in a grid whose rows depend only on the header's width, so changing numbers never move the view), the Staff list (names dragged between the roles' boxes, tapped to follow; the mine's crew list's markup and styles) and a Lab button (a tap on the fast-forwarding note or the fire notice fades it away, `fadeAway` in `ui/fade-away.ts`: a fire's cost is forgotten, its alarm and the note stay down until the fire is out or the catching up done), and pays Knowledge for actual simulated steps. Time away (up to 24 hours) is banked in `idleMs`, spent in 0.1-second steps at up to `IDLE_SPEED` (120 times normal speed), within a 6 ms frame budget. While more than `BOOST_FROM` (2 s) is owed, each step spends `IDLE_BOOST` (10) steps' worth of it and pays ten times its Knowledge (`idleDrain` in `src/away.ts`): the simulation runs no faster, the bank just runs down and pays out ten times as fast; the Mine does the same with ten ticks a tick, its Smithy points boosted with the fractions carried (`boosted`). The Mine spends the same full-day bank tick by tick, also with a 6 ms frame budget; neither pays an offline rate estimate. Both display remaining time and persist unused time across closing. Library daylight follows its simulated clock (`day`), turning a fifth as fast (`DAY_SLOWDOWN`) while more than `FAST_FORWARD` (5 s) is owed, so fast-forwarded days don't race past. `awayKnowledge` and `away` keep the growing account for the welcome screen. Optional Library save fields also retain pixel fire state (heat, fuel, wetness, char, soot, smoke, ash and droplets), damaged shelves, charred and loose books, water butts, crew positions and injuries, counters, simulation time, minute phase and random state; malformed optional data is ignored. **Shared play-time sky.** `daylight.ts` owns the five-minute visible day (`DayClock`, `skyAtTime`, `worldSky`) shared by Defend/Ambush/Siege, the Mine, Library and Tiles windows. `main.ts` advances it only in visible foreground frames, resets timing on visibility/page lifecycle events, and saves optional `Save.dayClock`; no time away is added to scenery. **Metal economy.** `src/metals.ts` shares prices, affordability, payment and Copper/Silver/Gold labels; `save.smithy` is the sole spendable metal wallet. Mine/Library hosts expose `metals` and `spendMetals`. Starting metal is 10 Copper; a second miner costs 2 Copper, the first shelf 1 and the first librarian 2. Mine level upgrades start at 4–6 Copper; later levels add Silver then Gold. Larger crews/collections add precious metals, and lab expansions start at 10 Copper before adding Silver/Gold. Bombs cost 1 Copper (`BOMB_PRICE`). Unlimited money bypasses affordability and spends nothing. DEFEND awards only one upgrade point per new highest wave; `payWave` records the best with payment so duplicate events cannot pay twice. Knowledge comes only from the Library. There is no battle Gold, Gold found, Plunder, Copperworks or Spoils topic. Run `tests/metals.test.ts`, `tests/progression.test.ts`, `tests/tiles.test.ts` and `node tests/metal-economy.mjs` (isolated desktop/mobile browser fixture).

**Quadratic balance and project work.** `src/economy.ts` holds price coefficients, used by Training, Forge, Mine buildings/hires and Library shelves/staff/lab. Training costs `1 + 5 * owned²` of its metal tier and takes `60 * next-rank²` seconds per smith, times its metal's `TIER_WORK` (`training-jobs.ts`: copper 1, silver 2, gold 4). `src/forge-jobs.ts` reserves mixed metals for all ordinary upgrades and War drums, taking `120 * next-rank²` seconds per smith times its bill's most precious metal (`billWork`); its one project shares named smiths with Training via `busySmiths`. `src/research-jobs.ts` reserves Knowledge for every skill and spell path rank, sharing all current researchers on one lab project: `300 * next-rank²` researcher-seconds or 24 researcher-hours for a crown. Zero workers pauses; completion applies effects exactly once; cancellation refunds the saved actual paid bill, including across dev toggles. `save.researchJob/researchClock` and `save.forgeJob/forgeClock` persist work; Training jobs retain `paid`. Main settles all clocks each second and after loading. Study/Smithy show time, staffing and refund controls. No Smithy upgrade is instant: Training and Forge always need a free smith and their time, whatever the dev options; Instantaneous research covers only the Study. The Smithy shows each bill as amounts by small ingot icons (`costHtml`, `.metal-icon`). Run `tests/upgrade-work.test.ts` and `node tests/upgrade-work.mjs`.
