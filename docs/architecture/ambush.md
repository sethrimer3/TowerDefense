# Ambush

The Ambush campaign: levels, tracks, outposts, economy, waves, validation and its sidebar/menu.

Moved from `AGENTS.md`; keep it current with the code it describes.

**Combat tab and campaigns.** The combat tab cycles Defend → Ambush → Siege without leaving navigation ID 'defend'; Defend's, Ambush's, Siege's and the Settings tab's icons are pixel art drawn in code (`ui/mode-icons.ts`: `modeIconPixels`, `modeIconUrl` a cached data URL, `modeIconImg` its `<img>`, which `TAB_ICONS` and `combatIcon` use; a crenellated stone tower with a blue heater shield and brass cross, a bow drawn from a bush with eyes in it, a trebuchet with a stone in flight and an iron cog with a brass collar meshed with a little brass one, 24 art pixels square, each material shaded from the upper left inside a black outline; `tests/mode-icons.test.ts`). Ambush is split into a level selector (campaign/ambush-ui.ts), a thin native battle page (ambush-battle-ui.ts), and pure mode rules (ambush-sim.ts, ambush-grid.ts, levels.ts). Thirty stable zone/level IDs own deterministic tracks, anchors, stars and unlock migration. `trackFor` (levels.ts) winds each lane down the board from an edge (top or a side) in seeded switchbacks, now and then a long diagonal, ending above the keep: one full-width lane, two from the tenth level (`laneCount`: halves winding down either side, meeting before the keep, an outpost on each) and three from the twenty-second (the third straight down the middle, then turning aside to join a half). Outposts sit 1/3 and 2/3 along a single lane, else 0.4 along each half, retried over seeded variants until clear of each other, the castle and the other half's road. Some levels (`RIVERS` by zone, all of the Crystal Caves) get a river across the board (`riverFor`: a meandering band 3–4 cells deep, clear of outposts, the castle, entries and every road running along it; levels with one have no diagonals, so every crossing is straight); battlefield.ts makes its cells native `WATER` (solid, so ground enemies cross only by the bridges) and the road cells over it `CityMap.bridges` (2 over the water, 1 on the banks), which `park-art.ts` runs the water under (no open water beneath) and `defend/bridge-art.ts` (`paintBridges`, after the ponds in `city-layer.ts`) draws as plank decks with railings. Defend maps have no `bridges`, so they draw as before. `defend/river-art.ts` reads the river's cells back from the map (`riverBed`: water outside the city and the bridges over it) and paints it as the forest's lakes are (`water-palette.ts`, shared with `forest-art.ts`'s `lake`): `riverShape` smooths the cells' steps into wobbling shores, `paintRiver` paints bank, outline, depth-dithered water, bank shadow, foam lip, lily pads and reeds (from `park-art.ts` into the city layer, bridges drawn over it, and again in the forest floor so its banks lie on the grass), and the river is no pond (no ducks, ripples or reflections). `RiverLife` (kept by the renderer, drawn after the forest's water) runs the current's streaks downstream, twinkles glints, rings the rain and swims fish shadows (`FISH`) that dart away from walkers and dive out of sight for a while; in the cold it ices over like the ponds; still under Reduce Motion or with effects off; nothing on the bridges. Presentation only. Run `tests/river-art.test.ts` and `node tests/river-life.mjs` (dev server required; fixture `tests/river-life.html`, no player saves). `field.lanes` holds every lane (`field.path` is the first); Outposts gives each post a mark on each lane passing within 8 cells, and each enemy keeps the lane it was released on (`assign`) or the one nearest where it is first seen. Phase 3 replaces numbered pads with free native quarter-grid placement: water, roads, rocks/boulders, spawn edge and the keep/castle area reject footprints; towns regenerate native houses around placed buildings, retaining their initial house count and damage. Outposts have native solid wooden fence cells at 10% stone-wall HP (`FENCE_SHARE`), their rails run along the ring's middle line so corners join (`Building.rails`, `fenceRails`), drawn all at once as stout pixel-art rails and posts like the wooden gate's (`drawFenceSections`, `paintFences`: a post at corners, every other cell and wherever the fence stops), standing on the open floor, not a stone wall's footing; in the forest the fence cells are open ground (`ForestPlan.open`), so its grass and blades grow under them, and the renderer draws the fences (with their own shadow) and the gates again over it; the star tree's Palisades III (`STONE_RANK`, `ambushField(level, { stone })`) walls them in native stone instead (`STONE_SHARE`). `campaign/ambush-field.ts` (kept apart from `battlefield.ts`, which Siege's calibration hash covers) finishes `battlefield.ts`'s layout: `gateSpots` sets a gate `ROAD_WIDTH` (3) cells long, as wide as the road (`setGate`: a native `"gate"` building, `Building.woodGate` in a fence) wherever a road crosses an outpost's ring or the castle's, widening the road to its passage; `wood-gate-art.ts` draws the wooden gate (posts, two plank leaves swinging inward) and `gateArtRect` centres a one-cell-deep gate's art on its line so the stone gatehouse straddles the wall, and a gate shorter than its art along it, so its posts or towers stand on the wall either side of the road. `ground-art.ts` paints street dirt under houses and wooden gates too (no gravel lots), and `groundArt(map, grassy)` (the forest's areas) lets the open ground's grass creep raggedly out over the roads. The entry arrow (`entry-arrow.ts`) is a small pixel arrow on the road's middle line, and the flier warning plays once from when it goes up (`WARNING_MS`, `AmbushBattle.warnedAt`), then holds still. A gate stands as long as one stretch of its wall, opens for the player's troops (native `stepGates`) and lets the enemy through while open; `Outposts.toll` (`ScenarioRules.toll`, `WALL_TOLL`, also read by `downhill` in scenario routes) prices breaking the walls beside it so the crowd batters the gate instead. Ground enemies come in by the gate, raze houses, then rejoin the track. Fliers use their native straight flight to the keep and ignore outpost breaches. campaign/outposts.ts owns the latched breach/standing-house health and 3/2/1 stars; keep death immediately defeats with zero stars. Native target flow fields and attack helpers are optional scenario adapters; ordinary Defend routes are unchanged. Native layout.structureClearance supplies the exact one-quarter ring. AmbushDrag extends EditSession so BoardPointers supplies Defend's ghost, raised touch pickup, magnifier, cancellation, camera and quarter-grid overlays. A tap inspects rather than moving the building. Hidden native lot IDs are allocated before DefendSim's arrays and remain stable through sales; selling withdraws only that home's garrison/timers. Native ForestArt clears trees under new foundations, and `ForestArt.cover` leaves its floor clear under each standing building's own sprite pixels (`spriteFootprint` from `city-layer.ts`; the whole lot under rubble), since the forest was planned before the lots were taken. In battle, as in Defend, a press on the picked troop building or its flag drags out its rally point (`AmbushBattlePage.aimBuilding`, drawn by `drawInspect`) and a double tap plants the free rally flag (`rallyTap`). Moving is allowed before waves; buying/selling works in battle. The base-only five-building palette, local gold upgrades and owned base-strength spells ensure no Defend inventory, Knowledge or Smithy power enters Ambush. ambush-waves.ts defines 20 deterministic crowd waves per level (placeholder until tuned by hand): `crowdSize` runs from 50 (first level, first wave) to 500 (last level, last wave); `crowdRoster` takes the zone's single-bodied species (no splitters, summoners, chains, fortresses, boats or regenerators) of native HP at most `crowdHp`; each wave leans on two to four of them, cheaper ones commoner, fliers only from wave 4 and at most 15%; waves 10 and 20 add the zone's toughest ground boss within `bossHp`. Every entry is released at its lane's entry over `release + count × releasePer` seconds with its HP (and any ranged shield) scaled so the wave's total is `waveHp` (armor halving physical damage counting double) (×`levelGrowth` 1.07 a level, ×`waveGrowth` 1.11 a wave) and its damage (`Enemy.raisedDamage`) so the average enemy deals `waveDps`. The keep (×`keepHp`, with base Arrow loops) and outposts (×`outpostHp`) are sturdier than Defend's and grow, like the opening gold, by `defenseGrowth` (1.05) a level, a little slower than the crowds. Send starts each wave explicitly; optional campaigns.ambush.autoSend remembers true only (default off), waits for a clear plus a three-second break, and early manual sends award nothing. Ambush reuses Defend’s saved speed/unlock. Its native slide-in sidebar, Defend's width with hidden scrollbars, fixes a small stone plaque (KeepBricks health, tiny 3/2/1 stars, gold) above the scrolling palette, its coin, stars and empty star slots pixel sprites from campaign/ambush-icons.ts (`iconImg`); its hide button (or the header's Buildings button) slides it away (`toggleSide`, no re-render) and a transparent gold-bordered box over the board's top left (`.ambush-mini`, a tap brings the sidebar back) keeps the health as OP1:/OP2:/Keep: with bricks and the gold; fallen outposts crack away a star, keep death gives zero, and the result panel offers Retry/Next/Select. ui/spell-bar.ts supplies native icons, select/cancel/Escape, recharge/ready cues and shared native aim overlays in all three combat modes. Defend’s existing Skills drag menu is retained; OtherPlans’ menu preference is independent follow-up work. flier-warning.ts draws the animated red dotted entry-to-keep line, ring and warning pixels, static under Reduce Motion. Old Ambush layouts are discarded, old clears become Mossbound one-star results, and only best-star improvements persist. Previews use the same map factory and zone through one native renderer with a bounded lazy cache. Optional scenario/native presentation helpers leave ordinary Defend goldens unchanged. Tests: ambush-grid.test.ts, ambush-levels.test.ts, ambush-economy.test.ts, campaign-native.test.ts; browser entrypoints ambush-placement.mjs, ambush-levels.mjs, ambush-previews.mjs, ambush-outposts.mjs, ambush-hud.mjs and ambush-spells.mjs (1280×800 and 390×844; screenshots in test-results/).

**Ambush economy.** campaign/economy.ts owns the opening gold (`startingGold(order)`: `STARTING_GOLD.base` 800, growing by `AMBUSH_WAVES.defenseGrowth` a level through the campaign) and native-catalog DPS estimates rounded to tens; kill bounties come from the waves (each wave pays `goldPerHp` times its HP, shared evenly between its kills, `AmbushWave.bounty`; the young of the fallen pay the current wave's). The five base kinds are available to everyone; Ambush uses zero Armory levels and no inherited Knowledge paths. Prices never drive combat. Purchases/sales work during waves on stable native homes. Refunds return the full purchase-plus-upgrade investment until wave 2 starts, then 70% rounded down to tens. ambush-upgrade-price.ts prices native nextSteps with bounded tier/DPS estimates, never Knowledge costs. Native per-home paths and kind changes apply in place during combat, preserving building HP fraction and retraining garrisons. Ultimate merges validate the new footprint and consume two real maxed partners, conserving all investments. Ambush snapshots owned spells at rank zero/base strength. AmbushTallies receives an optional native post-mitigation/clamped damage sink; burn, blaze, venom, bleed and delayed projectiles retain their source home. hurtEnemy’s final optional credit argument is statistics-only: DoT attribution must never replace the native physical origin or alter directional armor. ambush-native-dots.test.ts verifies this against Siege Beetles with statistics on/off. Defend allocates no tally map and its replay remains unchanged. The sidebar portrait, stats and local upgrades refresh at most four times per second. Native deaths pay once, excluding escapes and fortress body parts; gold may be fractional and shows rounded down. Retry starts a fresh wallet and empty grid. Gold never enters Mine metals or Siege. Ambush-only permanent ranks live in optional campaigns.ambush.ranks; ambush-upgrades.ts derives a star pool from best results minus ranked costs (maximum 90), bounds imported spending and supplies nine independent branches. Prices are 2/3/5 stars per branch, except Head start at 3/7 for one/two free Archers. Undo/respec are free; free buildings have zero resale investment. Damage, HP, prices, bounties, refunds and spell cooldowns snapshot from this tree alone. AmbushStarTree reuses UpgradeGraph/TreeMotes and MagicCircle, offers a named branch picker, and disposes graph/circle loops when closed or leaving the selector. First-wave sends increment optional bounded per-level attempt counts; losses never lower best stars. tests/ambush-economy.test.ts checks accounting/isolation; tools/ambush-prices.ts regenerates docs/design/AMBUSH_ECONOMY.md. Prices and rewards remain rough estimates. See docs/design/AMBUSH_BALANCE.md.

**Ambush validation.** tools/ambush-balance.ts emits deterministic JSONL from synthetic saves (--levels, --seconds, --buildings, --simple, --earned-stars/--max-stars, --debug, and overrides of the wave curve's knobs such as --hp, --dps, --gold-per-hp and --start-gold). docs/design/AMBUSH_BALANCE.md records the placeholder curve and all 30 outcomes; AMBUSH_PERFORMANCE.md records the paired native 20-wave/3× phone-viewport benchmark. Native boss waves 10/20, target-cell road arrival, nonempty zone rosters, zone star totals, BattleSound and reduced-motion-safe victory sparks are complete. Tests include ambush-complete-route.test.ts and ambush-native-dots.test.ts; browser checks include ambush-polish.mjs and ambush-performance.mjs.

**Ambush top bar upgrades.** In Ambush the building menu's top bar holds the building's next upgrades instead of the tree button (`BuildingMenuSpec.upgrades`, `MenuUpgrade`, hook `data-bm-upgrade`; `PlacedMenuHost.upgrades/upgrade`), each with its gold cost above it (`.bm-cost`, red while short, `setMenuGold`; `buildingMenuBox`'s `headroom`, `MENU_COST_ROOM`, keeps room for it). The first tap marks it and the sidebar (widened, `.ambush-side.offering`) shows its card (`#ambush-offer`: sprite, cost, Tap again to buy, description, bullet list of changes from `campaign/ambush-offer.ts`'s `offerText`, `statChanges` diffing `kindStats` on a fresh save); a second tap on the option (or the card's button) buys it and `PlacedBuildingMenu.updateUpgrades` redraws only the top bar. Holding a building still opens its stats panel. Run `node tests/ambush-top-upgrades.mjs` (dev server required; desktop and phone).
