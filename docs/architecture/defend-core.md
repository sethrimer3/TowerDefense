# Defend core

The battle simulation, its exact arithmetic, targeting and contact rules, the city layout, and the Defend page.

Moved from `AGENTS.md`; keep it current with the code it describes.

**Defend** is the game, self-contained in `src/defend/` with its own save slice (`save.defend`) and page (`DefendPage`, `defend/ui.ts`); see `docs/DEFEND.md`. Its battle simulation, `DefendSim` (`defend/sim.ts`), owns the world state and what happens to it (damage, rebuilding, blasts, movement), and steps the units in a fixed order. Each unit type's decisions live in its own module: `enemies.ts`, `troops.ts` (barracks, swordsmen, archers; the Mage Guild trains its elemental mages and the Valkyrie palace its valkyries the same way), `mages.ts` (fire mages throwing `sim.fireballs`, which burst through `sim.explode` sparing your own people and leave `sim.blazes`, burning ground that hurts ground enemies a second; a burn passes `hurtEnemy(e, n, false)` so it doesn't flash), `valkyries.ts` (valkyries making charge stabs: a blink along the line toward an enemy, as far as `stabLength` or until `blocked` by something solid, hurting every enemy within `STAB_WIDTH` of it, leaving `sim.stabs` and an optional `guard` on the soldier during which neither enemies nor blasts hurt her, absent when unset), `dark-wizards.ts` (the dark wizard keep's four corner turrets, `DarkKeeps`, at `turretSpots`, and its one dark wizard, `stepDarkWizard`, both loosing black lightning through `chainBolt`: a bolt strikes its target, then leaps to the nearest enemy not yet struck within `chainJump`, forking back from the latest struck that still has one, up to `turretChain`/`wizardChain` enemies; `sim.bolts` keeps each bolt's points and `from` links), `siege.ts` (the self-driving siege engines, rolling cannons, ballistas, firework launchers, trebuchets, great bombards and dragonfire batteries, each an `EnemyDef` with a `siege` entry: an engine marches like any enemy and, with the keep or the building its own flow-field way runs into (`blockerAhead`) or a defender within range, braces (`SIEGE_SETUP`) and shoots `sim.siegeShots`, balls, stones, piercing bolts or rocket volleys that hurt only the city and its people; `aim` is absent while it rolls, `siegeShots` hashes in replays only when present), `boats.ts` (the magic boats, Enchanted Skiff, Spellbound Sloop, Arcane Galleon and Deluge Ark, each an `EnemyDef` with a `boat` entry: a boat sails straight at the keep through the ground (`stepBoat`), dropping pools of its water (`sim.floods`, each holding then drying by `floodRadius`, found through a per-tile index by `wetAt`) whose reach sinks every house and structure (`sim.sink`, recorded in `sim.sinkings` for the art; the galleon also sinks wall stones, the ark the keep) and ramming what it can't sink; `stepFloods` soaks fire mages and puts out blazes, `fizzles` stops blasts centred in water (`explode`, `hostileBurst`, fireballs) and `sheltered` spares whoever stands in it from splash and fire; both lists are empty in runs without boats, and `ageWater` dries them after a loss), `boat-art.ts` (the boats as outlined pixel sprites per heading, foam and rune light), `flood-art.ts` (`FloodArt`: the water as pond-style pixel art with the bank mirrored in, rings, crests and sparkles, walls rising out of it, buildings sinking into it), `siege-art.ts` (the engines as outlined pixel sprites at `SP` pixels a cell, one cached sprite per aim direction and frame, their shots and the rockets' `firework` effects), `war-banner.ts` (`WarBanner`, `sim.warBanner`, set by `plantBanner`: the item the player plants mid-battle; while it stands, every troop's step hands over to `answerBanner` when nothing is in its own reach, marching down the banner's flow field (`fillFlowField`, standing buildings at a high cost) and closing on enemies within `RALLY_REACH` of it; null in every run without one, so replays are untouched; the page's drag is `{ from: "banner", placed? }`, whose `Drop` says whether a planted banner was tapped, and the renderer draws it with `drawWarBanner`, the keep's `flagPixels`), city gates (`layout.ts`: `GateSpot`, a city tile and a `Side`, saved in the layout's `gates`, legal where the wall runs (`gateOk`), `gateRect` the middle 3 cells of the edge the wall's 2 deep, `placeGate`/`moveGate`/`removeGate`, dropped back to the palette when a tile edit takes the wall away; `fitLayout` keeps the cells inside clear and `generateCity` paves them and adds each gate as a `"gate"` building after the wall stones; the sim's `ownSolid` is `solid` with standing gates open, used by the city's own people's routes and steps (`followPath`, `moveToward`'s `own`), the war banner's field and the valkyries' charge, and is `solid` itself without gates so replays are untouched; `gate-art.ts` draws the gatehouse per side and door `frame`, the renderer swinging the doors open while a soldier or civilian is near; carried by `{ from: "gate" }` drags keyed by edge, `nearestEdge`, overlay `gates`), the wall's own defenses (`wall-defenses.ts`: wall spikes, `layout.ts`'s `spikes` on gate-like edges but not a gate's, `spikesRect` the wall's outer row along the edge, which `generateCity` lists as `map.spikes` (absent without any) and `stepSpikes` turns into a melee cut of `spikeDamage` every `spikeEvery` seconds (the Smithy's `spikeDamage` and `spikeRate` levels; Wall spikes is its own topic with three Study paths) for each enemy on foot touching a standing stone's stakes; wall ballistas, `layout.ts`'s `ballistas` of `CornerSpot`s, legal where the wall turns through a right angle (`cornerOk`: one or three of the corner's four tiles city), a 2 × 2 `"wallBallista"` bastion building in the wall's stones there (`ballistaRect`, `corner` with its `out` facing), shooting through `Towers` (`shootBallista`) piercing bolts, `sim.ballistaBolts` and `sim.ballistaAim`, all absent or empty in a city without them so replays are untouched; `wall-defense-art.ts` draws the stakes into the city layer under the wall stones (so only their iron points show, past the brick face on the south), the bastion with damage and rubble, and the crossbow per heading live (`drawBallistas`); carried by `{ from: "spikes" }` and `{ from: "ballista" }` drags, `carriesEdge`/`carriesCorner`, `nearestCorner`, `wallSpotRect`; `tests/defend-wall-defenses.test.ts`), `bait.ts` (monster bait, a structure every enemy goes for before the keep: while any stack stands (`baitStanding`), ground enemies march `sim.baitField`, a flow field filled from every standing stack beside the keep's (`marchField`), and fliers, boats, blink imps and siege engines head for or shoot the nearest stack (`lureOf`), and houses lure nobody; `sim.baitFalls` counts each stack's falls, which `restockable` holds against Restocking (`baitRestock`) for the civilians, and with Powder kegs (`baitBlast`) `baitFell` bursts it and leaves a blaze; a run without bait has an empty `baitField` and plays as before), `civilians.ts` (Repair shop workers reserve whole buildings, stand beside them and restore a percentage of maximum HP), `towers.ts` and `wizard.ts` (wizard towers alternating `sim.flames` and `sim.frosts`; an enemy's optional `chill` slows it, and stays absent when unset so runs without ice hash exactly as before). `pathing.ts` holds the pure grid A*, the enemies' flow field and the collision test. A sim takes the Armory's levels and, optionally, `Bonuses` (`catalog.ts`: multipliers on troop HP and damage, drill time, tower damage and reload, wall and keep HP, rebuild time and bomb damage; `NO_BONUSES` is all 1, so a sim without them plays exactly as before), and counts the enemies slain by kind (`sim.slain`, which the page pays for each frame; not part of the replayed state). `sim.stats` (`battle-stats.ts`, `BattleStats`) tallies each wave (difficulty, enemies sent and slain, damage dealt, damage taken by the city and the keep, troops, civilians and buildings lost, time), also outside the replayed state; when the keep falls the page shows it on the parchment banner (`defeat.ts`, `defeatHTML`: stat tiles and a small SVG bar chart a measure, one bar a wave), which a tap anywhere, on it or the board, or its close button, closes for the build phase. `DefendRenderer` (`defend/render.ts`) owns the camera and the order of the drawing passes: `city-layer.ts` paints the cached static city, with the streets as dirt pixel art (`ground-art.ts`, `groundArt`: the streets and the player's structures' lots in the city, so a sprite smaller than its lot stands on the street rather than a square of bare gravel, while a structure outside the city stands on the open floor with no lot at all; blotchy browns darker at the kerbs, grass growing out unevenly over the edges beside parks and bare dirt biting back into them, both along one wavering line (`edge`) so they never meet at the cells' boundary, `bare` marking those park pixels so the live grass keeps off and `turf` the street pixels grassed over, where it grows too) and the shadows (`shadow-art.ts`: `heights` stands houses up as gabled roofs, the wall taller with its sloping face, the keep, structures (each by its own sprite's solid pixels, `Footprint`, read once a kind, size, look and `pixelKey()` by `city-layer.ts`'s `spriteFootprint`, its lot only in Node or before it is drawn; a gate by its two towers alone, its passage open) and tree canopies, and `shadowMask` casts one crisp mask from the upper left over it, so the wall's shadow falls on the roofs beside it, `paintShadows` filling it a run at a time with each art pixel's edges rounded like the buildings' cells so no lit sliver opens between a wall and its shadow; fallen buildings cast nothing); it re-exports the pond and tree geometry of `park-geometry.ts`, `pondDisc`/`pondField`/`pondPath`/`POND`, `hasTree`/`treeCanopy`, that the live effects line up with. both skipped when the `effectsOff` setting is on), `fences.ts`, `lighting.ts` the torchlight and shadows (each frame's pools and hand torches summed in `light-field.ts`), `ground-relief.ts` (`GroundRelief`: the flagstones' bump map outside the city, four facing masks baked from the floor art's brightness by `paintFloor` in `city-layer.ts`, lit per light through cached weight sprites; fixed lights baked per map version, moving ones per frame), `battle-art.ts` each frame's damage, scorches, units and effects (a swordsman's strike drawn as a half-circle sword swing with a lunge and a fading trail, timed from its cooldown so the sim state is untouched), `dark-art.ts` (the dark wizard's sprite and staff, `drawDarkWizard`; black lightning rasterized into one board-sized pixel buffer, `BoltBuffer`/`rasterBolts`, black cores in crimson glow, only the changed box cleared and copied, drawn by `DarkArt` once a frame; `darkLights`), `valkyrie-art.ts` (the valkyries as cached sprites with their spears and guard halo, `drawValkyrie`; the core's call draws every part), `wizard-art.ts` (`WizardArt`: the wizard towers' fire and the ice wave's crystal shards as pixel art, the fire's puffs inside one black outline, the shards' facets shaded into an ice palette and baked per cluster and size step, advanced on battle time; Park life is presentation only: it never changes the city or the battle, and draws its randomness (rain rings) from `defendRandom("effects")`. All units draw from one seeded random stream, so the step order and each unit's draws decide a replay. The page's Wave button opens the starting wave picker (`wave-picker.ts`, `wavePickerHTML`: every wave up to `waveReach`, the best wave or `unlockedWave`, with its `waveDifficulty` and a bar, boss waves marked); the choice is saved as `startWave` (`startingWave` keeps it in reach) and a run starts there through `sim.startAt`, which only sets the wave before the first step, so replays are untouched. `tests/defend-replay.test.ts` steps fixed cities, upgrade levels, seeds, bombs and demolitions through `update()` and hashes the whole sim state every 5 s against `tests/fixtures/defend-replay.golden.json`, reporting when a run first diverges. Regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended gameplay change. The battle must play the same in every JavaScript engine and Node version (CI runs Node 24), so battle code uses only `+ - * /` and `Math.sqrt`, with `intPow`, `sq` and `dist` from `src/exact.ts`: no `Math.pow`/`**`, `hypot`, `exp`, `log` or trigonometry (the wizard aims by unit vectors and tests cones by slope). Drawing may use `Math` freely. Check a replay change under both: `npx -y node@24 --experimental-transform-types --import ./tests/pin-random.ts --test tests/defend-replay.test.ts`. The header shows the keep's health as a wall of bricks (`keep-bricks.ts`, `KeepBricks`: two courses on a small canvas; as HP drops the bricks crack and break away from the right in tumbling pieces, presentation only, from `defendRandom("effects")`) and opens the enemy journal (`journal.ts`: `paintJournal` draws its button, a skull-marked bestiary with a wax seal while there are unread entries; each entry has a portrait, `journal-portrait.ts` (`paintPortraits`), the enemy drawn by the battle's own `drawEnemyArt` on a stand-in with a stub sim, trimmed and scaled up inside a pixel-art oak frame with brass corners). On the page, the board's canvas fills its view and the palette (`☰ Build`, in battle `☰ Items`) slides in beside it, headed while building by a category button (`PALETTE_CATEGORIES`, `ITEM_CATEGORY`, `inCategory` in `catalog.ts`: All, Towers, Infantry, City) whose list opens inline; the city tile's palette icon is `tile-art.ts` (`cityTilePixels`, the board's dirt, roofs and a tree); `DefendRenderer.resize(refW, refH)` takes the board's scale from the whole stage, so it holds while the view narrows (`boardSize(px)` in `grid.ts` is the board's extent in canvas pixels, which board-space passes use rather than the canvas's size), and the camera centres the board along an edge where the view is wider. Board pointers go through `board-pointers.ts` (`BoardPointers`), a small state machine: idle, viewing (one pointer pans, two pinch-zoom), or dragging a palette item, structure, keep, city tile or bomb under a ghost icon; a lone press that lets go without panning is a `tap`. While the Gameplay setting Carry dragged items above the finger (`raiseDrag`; until the player sets it, `defaultSettings` turns it on where `(pointer: coarse)` matches, `touchFirst`, and off under a mouse) is on, a held item rides `RAISE` cells (four quarter tiles) above the pointer, so the finger doesn't hide where it goes: `BoardPointers.at` lifts the drag's cell and its ghost icon by `raise` (the host's `raiseDrag`), clamped to the board's top row while the pointer is over the board (which still decides `overBoard` and `overPalette`); browser tests let go that far below their target through `tests/drag-raise.mjs` (`raiseOf`, `dropAt`). While the Gameplay setting Magnifying glass while placing (`magnifier`, off by default) is on, `defend/loupe.ts`'s `Loupe` shows a brass-rimmed box above the finger (beside it when there is no room above) copying the board canvas's last frame round the drag's cell at `LOUPE_ZOOM` times, nearest-neighbour, with a crosshair at its middle: `BoardPointers` points it at every held drag and at `BoardAim` gestures (centred on their optional `focus`, in cells, else the pointer), and its public `magnify(pointer, cell)` lets any other gesture (rally flags, tower crosshairs) point it, `magnify(null)` hiding it; the page calls `drawLoupe` after drawing the board each frame. Presentation only. A press on a city tile (or the keep's tile away from the keep: `EditSession.byTile`) only lifts it once held still for `LIFT_MS` (500 ms, `BoardPointers.pend`); until then it is an ordinary view press, so moving first pans and letting go first taps, and `liftProgress` hands the renderer `DrawOptions.lifting`, a black-outlined gold square `drawLifting` grows from the tile's middle to its edges. It only turns pointers into board cells; a tap (or, while building, a lifted structure let go where it stood, a `Drop` with `tap`, which leaves the layout as it was) picks out a tower or troop building through `inspect.ts` (`inspectable`, `reachOf`: the towers' ranges from the same `archerTowerRange`, `cannonTowerRange`, `wizardReach` and `watchReach` the battle uses, as circles; `troopsOf`; `drawInspect` frames it and its troops in gold, `DrawOptions.inspect`), and after a lost run starts the rebuilding; each drag is an `EditSession` (`edit-session.ts`), a pure transaction over the layout it started from: what a press lifts (`EditSession.lift`), the legal tiles and the layout each makes, the overlay shown while held, and the `Drop` a release makes, which the page applies. `tests/defend-edit-session.test.ts` drives whole drags in Node. `drag-rules.ts` holds the rules behind it (unit-tested in `tests/defend-drag-rules.test.ts`). The city itself comes from `layout.ts` (the player's tiles and structures; the board is a square of 12 × 12 tiles (`TILES_W`, `TILES_H` in `grid.ts`, shared by Defend, Ambush and Siege), and nothing is built on its outer ring: city tiles, structures and wall pieces stand only on the middle 10 × 10, the buildable field (`buildableTile`, `BUILD_MARGIN`), the ring left open for the enemies to come in by; the retired Outlying districts skill's Knowledge is refunded on load, `OUTSKIRTS_KNOWLEDGE` in `save.ts`); any city tile but the keep's, pressed away from its buildings, lifts with everything on it (`tileMovable`, `moveCityTile`: structures keep their uids, quarters and paths, its gates and spikes go along, ballistas on corners it made, and what no longer fits the new wall drops back to the palette), except under a structure spanning several tiles; `fitLayout` checks where the player put each structure (no search, no randomness): a tile is `SUB` (8) cells, so the board's grid of quarter tiles (`QUARTER` 4 a tile, `QC` 2 cells each) is whole cells; a structure is saved at its tile (`tx`, `ty`) and top left quarter (`qx`, `qy`), and its footprint (`footprint` in `catalog.ts`, whole quarters, never rotated; `quarterSize`, with a smaller `compact` footprint behind a building-specific upgrade that the layout's `compact` kinds name) must lie inside its tile, off the wall and clear of a gate's way in, and every building but monster bait (`huddles`) keeps one clear quarter all round from the others; the keep stands 2 × 2 quarters in its tile's middle (`keepQuarter`); `fitLayout`'s reason names what is wrong. `placeStructureAt`/`moveStructureTo` place at a quarter; `arrange`/`settle` search every arrangement of a tile's structures (bounded backtracking) and are used only where the game has to move things itself: old saves, carried tiles and a city tile placed under structures, so a tile takes whatever any arrangement fits. An upgrade that changes a building's footprint takes it up to the palette (`standing` in `specializations.ts`); `sized` does the same for a compact upgrade. **Simulation target selection.** `DefendSim.nearestEnemy` visits the native cell/list order directly, preserving live HP/untargetable/armor exclusions, inclusive range edges and caller-specific first/last ties. Mages, Valkyries, ordinary ranged troops and non-volley archer towers avoid allocating a shortlist and computing distances twice; explicit flat-shot pools, Sky Archers' global list, secondary/area attacks and volleys keep their original queries. `pathing.firstSorted` retains only the best three candidates in stable comparator order for patrol/hunt/seek, including toughest-first patrols. `blocked` tests the four native terrain corners in order without allocating nested arrays. `tests/defend-performance.test.ts` checks query/corner parity and bounded stable selection; `tests/simulation-targeting-performance.ts` freezes the entire source at `6707b9f`, compares active native mixed/siege combat state and next RNG draws, and measures target/terrain components separately. The shared stress fixture accepts an optional simulation constructor for reference comparisons. Run the full suite/build and native browser stress scene; replay goldens stay unchanged. See `docs/SIMULATION_PERFORMANCE.md`.

**Defender targeting and transient buffers.** `defender-index.ts` buckets friendly units in four-cell bins only during the enemy phase, with live HP checks and original array-order tie breaking; small crowds and out-of-phase calls keep the linear scan. Rebuild before each indexed enemy phase, and end indexing before recruitment or friendly movement. Health-bar selection keeps an ordered top eight and only tracks peak strength when bars are hidden. `FloodArt` writes directly into reusable ImageData backing arrays, growing capacity at most to board size. `tests/defend-query-performance.ts` runs paired targeting/health-bar microbenchmarks; defender-index tests compare against the original scan, and render-cache tests compare original water pixels across pool resizing and settings.

**Simulation contact work.** `ice-motion.ts` submits active ground-body X/Y substeps to the existing crowd gate together, preserving the original full-X terrain check and checking full Y after crowd-clamped X. This removes a duplicate body-index rewrite while retaining native bucket order, including blocked/zero axes; non-ground/stale bodies and friendly movement keep their original path. `DefenderIndex` optionally builds separate body-contact buckets for worm enemy phases, including cloaked/perched people, canonical defender sizes, contact epsilon and soldier-then-civilian order. `DefendSim.contactDefenders` uses those only inside the indexed phase; standalone callers see live arrays. Ordinary targeting stays unchanged. `sweepAway` repeats worm-run classification only after processing a death; the initial pass and all cascade passes remain native. `tests/simulation-performance.ts` freezes `801e46c` and compares complete moving fixed steps, exact state/RNG and cleanup without rendering.
