# Defend play, spells and enemies

Battle controls, wave progress, areas, strike spells, war banner, enemies (fortresses, boats, zone rosters) and developer controls.

Moved from `AGENTS.md`; keep it current with the code it describes.

**Defend controls.** Build/Skills and Tiles/Abandon sit at the left; Start Defense or separate wave and keep-health boxes are centered; starting wave/battle speed sits before the journal and settings at the right. The wave box includes a demon icon and remaining enemies (queued plus living enemies, excluding fortress parts). The keep health appears only in the header; its damage flash follows the sprite silhouette. `civilians.ts` caches reachable home regions by map version; builders stranded outside without any route indoors panic, run to a reachable board edge at 1.8 times walking speed, and despawn only after leaving it, alive and without a death count. Run `tests/defend-civilians.test.ts` and `node tests/defend-controls.mjs` for this behavior and desktop/mobile header and keep-flash pixels. The starting-wave button and every row of the wave picker wear their zone's wall caps under a dark overlay: `ui/wall-tiles.ts` (`installWallTiles`, from `DefendPage`) puts each area's seamless 8 × 8 cell tile (`wallTilePixels` in `area-wall-art.ts`) on the root as `--wall-<area>`, `areaStyle` points `--area-wall` at it and rows shift it by `--wall-at`; zone headings and wave names carry a one-pixel orthogonal black text outline. The dialogs' wax seal is pixel art (`ui/seal-art.ts`, `sealPixels`, `installSeal` from main as `--seal-art`, outlined in code); Show legacy graphics (`:root.legacy-graphics`) brings back `seal.svg`. `DefendSave.battleSpeed` persists the chosen 1�/2�/unlocked 3� speed and is restored on each run; legacy or invalid values default to 1�. The enemy journal groups discovered species into each eligible zone, including offspring, sorted by difficulty. Defeat reports close only via their X or bottom Close button, with compact charts and a fixed footer.

**Starting-wave progress.** `DefendSave.furthestWave` records the highest entered wave, independently of cleared `bestWave` and `payWave` rewards. `recordWaveReached` handles new records on real `waveStart` events in `DefendPage.handleEvents`, selects `max(1, reached - 1)` and persists immediately, including a run that later loses or is abandoned. Equal/earlier waves leave manual `startWave` selections intact. `waveReach` permits starts through reached minus one, plus developer `unlockedWave`; developer unlocks and Unit tester never manufacture real reached progress. Decoding old saves infers reached as cleared best + 1 to preserve previously available starts, migrates the old wave-1 default to the previous reached wave, keeps other explicit selections (including wave 1 in current saves), and defaults missing/invalid selections to the previous reached wave. The picker states the actual starting wave; browser checks advance the real simulation until enemies spawn at that wave. Records clamp to `MAX_START_WAVE + 1`, starts to `MAX_START_WAVE`. Tests: `defend-wave-progress.test.ts`, `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE=/usr/bin/chromium node tests/defend-wave-progress.mjs` (desktop/phone picker, real-page persistence, defeat/reload, reward separation and tester guard).

Presentation only. Across the top third a great opening in the roof (`CAVE_OPENING`, `openingEdge`: a ragged cave mouth of bays and spurs from layered value noise, past the board's sides too; no ceiling holes under it) lets the sky in: `openingLight` dithers its edge, the floor under it is drawn from the sunlit copy cut to that mask (sliding with the sun like the patches, padded `OPEN_PAD` past the board), with moonlight over it by night and dust (`openingDustAt`) glinting in its light. The holes' light follows the shared sky's sun (`shaftLight`: from `opts.sun` while the world clock runs, else the fixed upper-left look `SHAFT_UP`): each patch slides `CAVE_CEILING` cells per cell of building shadow away from under its hole, drawn into the floor from a sunlit copy of it (`sunlitFloor`) cut to the patch's shape (only floor shows it), and the beam leans back toward the sun, long and slanting when it is low, short and steep at noon; still under Reduce Motion. Selected starting waves preview their own area; the renderer crossfades cached city layers over three seconds at an area boundary, keeping live damage and rebuilding consistent in both layers. Every area after Mossbound Ruins draws its wall as pixel art from `area-wall-art.ts` (`wallCapSprite`, `wallFaceSprite`: 16 art pixels a cell, each cell a dressed cap stone, whole or split, lit from the upper left, with the area's dressing: sandstone strata and drifts, glowing basalt joints, wet slate with crystal glints, mushrooms, snow and icicles, amethyst, glassy obsidian, gilded star stone, the void's runes) and its fallen stones in the area's tones (`wallRubbleTones`); standing-wall geometry still owns joins, breaches, outlines (a black line also where the top meets the hanging south face, and the corner pixel filled on inside corners) and the hanging south face. City walls are drawn top-down by default: no south face, the stone's own outline along its foot. The Settings page's Legacy Walls (`legacyWalls`, off by default; `setLegacyWalls`/`wallFaces` in `city-layer.ts`, set by main, repainting the city layer through its art listeners) brings back the hanging brick face (`paintWallFace`, `hangingFaces`, spikes' south reach). Presentation only: combat contact never includes the face. `tests/defend-area-walls.test.ts` checks the art is opaque, distinct per area and stable. `GroundRelief` invalidates by area as well as scale and loaded art. `weather.ts` rolls on entering an area: moss has 30% rain, Frozen Vault snow (a blizzard on a `STORM_CHANCE` roll or at night, drawn under the units), Amber Desert a sandstorm, Fungal Hollow mist, and other dry/underground areas no rain. `atmosphere.ts` (`Atmosphere`, `sim.atmosphere`, null outside those two areas) steps mist and sand per cell at 10 Hz on a wrapping board: wind (`windAt`) eases between held headings, blasts and projectiles swirl it (`disturb`, `wake`), mist seeps through walls below 75% HP, sand only through breaches, the wind always blows down and to the left (`WIND`), walkers stir mist but never move sand, an open gate (`sim.gateOpen`) lets sand and mist through, and city sand past `CLEANUP_DEPTH` is the civilians' lowest-priority job (`jobKind: "sand"`), and sand banked against a building (`press`, once a second: each open city cell beside it deeper than `SAND_PRESS.depth` adds its excess) past `SAND_PRESS.min` crushes it, so a long-open breach collapses houses and structures (never walls, gates, ballista bastions or the keep); leaving the desert clears it. The sim's dune field (`duneAt`: transverse crests `DUNE_WAVE` cells apart, repeating every `DUNE_PERIOD`) holds still (`DUNE_SPEED` 0, so `Atmosphere.drift` stays at 0) and sets how much sand lies outside the city and its wall; a fallen wall stone is not open ground, so sand reaches it (and the city) only through a breach that goes all the way out, and the dunes are never drawn over the wall's stones or their rubble. What is drawn is simpler: `atmosphere-art.ts`'s own surface (`duneHeights`: a few long, low, rounded swells `SWELL` cells apart, at most `DUNE_RELIEF` art pixels tall, gently bent, tiling the same period) baked into a sprite at eight pixels a cell (`dunePixels`: all one `SAND_COLOR`, each pixel only lighter or darker by its slope, lit `BUMP` times as steep as it stands, and the swells' cast shadows, `castShadows`, when the sun is low; looked up per slope bin from a shading table) tiled over the open ground outside the city, its wall and its gates. `easeDuneLight` turns the shading to the shared sky's sun (`Sun` from `daylight.ts`: heading snapped to 32 steps, its height to 20, contrast falling to 0.3 when the sun is down, no shadows then; fixed in the upper left without a sky), sweeping the next light's shadows a few lines a frame and showing it only once done, so a relight never stalls a frame (`setDuneLight` relights at once). The Settings page's HD Sand (`hdSand`, off by default, through the host and `DrawOptions.hdSand`, `setDuneStyle`) draws the older detailed dunes instead: the sim's `duneAt` shapes `HD_RELIEF` tall with wind ripples, dithered in `HD_TONES`, turned with the same heading. The desert's floor gets no flagstone `GroundRelief` (its light over the dunes showed as squares at night). `AtmosphereArt.drawGround` draws the dunes, their marks, the city's sand and the tumbleweeds from `render.ts`'s `drawCity`, before the battle's tint and darkness, so they darken at night and take the day's colours like the rest of the board; the wall's hanging faces are copied back over them (`drawFaces`). The Fungal Hollow's mist is still drawn after the lighting (`draw`). `SandMarks` (presentation only, pure typed arrays) keeps height `delta`s over the board in `CHUNK` (16) pixel chunks: trails `print`ed by walkers (a bounded few hundred a frame, only where the swell stands above `FLOOR`) and `crater`s from new `boom` effects, both healed by the wind every few battle seconds; only dirty chunks are repainted (`paintChunk`, at least `PAINT_PER_FRAME`, catching up within a few frames after a relight or drift; only marked pixels and those beside them, the rest left clear so the tile shows) and uploaded. No sand is drawn over the city wall. City sand (only the cells holding any) is drawn at the dunes' own eight pixels a cell, each drift a low rise (`DRIFT_RELIEF`) lit and coloured by the same sun, banding and style (HD too) as the dunes outside, without their cast shadows (`duneColor`'s `cast`); mist (dithered) is pixel art at four pixels a cell, plus outlined tumbleweed sprites (`tumbleweedPixels`, `WEED_PX` sizes; `Atmosphere.weeds` roll downwind, slide along walls, roll across the wind when pinned and come back in from the upwind edges); `tests/defend-atmosphere.test.ts`, `node tests/defend-dunes.mjs` (dev server required; isolated stress fixture, screenshots in `test-results/`). `wave-picker.ts` groups and colors waves by area. `area-enemies.ts` restricts wave selection to themed rosters; Nadir permits every non-hatched species. Offspring remain parent-only, budgets and multipart caps still apply. Nadir reuses crypt textures under a dark violet floor wash. Frozen Vault includes ships plus Ice Golems (double fire/explosion damage via the explicit damage element) and Sliding Ice Cubes (`ice-enemies.ts`: cardinal slides, bounded persistent trails, no blast deflection). `ice-enemy-art.ts` caches native pixel sprites; journal, kill accounting and discovery cover both. Run `tests/defend-zone-enemies.test.ts` and `node tests/defend-ice-enemies.mjs` for behavior and the isolated art/trail gallery. `tests/defend-areas.test.ts` checks boundaries, weather, fades and assets; run `node tests/defend-areas.mjs` with a dev server (optional `TEST_URL` and browser environment variables) to check browser pixels, mobile controls and direct starts using isolated fixtures without player-save access. `tools/prepare-area-art.ps1` crops supplied floors.

**War banner research.** The Skills palette lets the banner be placed immediately, then enforces a battle-time cooldown of 10 seconds (Rapid deployment reduces it to 7, 4 and 1). Its standalone Study → Spells → War banner Knowledge tree (`ui/banner-tree.ts`, rendered with the existing `UpgradeGraph` by `Ledger`) has no Ballistics prerequisite. The graph survives selection redraws and is destroyed during timed research or on topic changes; its selected node shows the existing research card, and completed ranks still drive the magic circle. The banner tree has shared cooldown, defense (10% less damage received) and influence radius (5 to 7 cells) upgrades before independent damage (+25%), marching speed (+50% while heading toward the banner) and life (+25% within influence, preserving health percentage on transitions) branches, with regeneration (5% of influenced maximum HP per second) after life. Existing War banner research remains a permanent troop damage bonus. `war-banner.ts` owns influence and health transitions; defender damage routes through `hurtDefender`. Research ranks use the existing skill save decoder. Placement ghosts and planted circles share the researched radius. Skills show a battle-time dark cooldown fill and gold ready outline. Run `tests/defend-war-banner.test.ts` and `node tests/defend-war-banner.mjs` (dev server required, isolated saves). **Strike spells and Necromancy.** Strike spells are lasting Skills (`tiles.ts` consumables, never used up) cast from the battle's Skills palette by dragging onto the board (`Drag` `{ from: "necromancy" }`, `Drop` `{ kind: "spell" }`). Each has its own Spells subject topic in `upgrade-subjects.ts` (`Topic.icon` stands in for a palette item): Smithy rows (`undeadHp`, `undeadDamage`, `graveTime`, the Training group `spells`, read as `Bonuses.undeadHp/undeadDamage/graveTime`; a row with `seconds` shows as a time) and Study paths in `knowledge-paths.ts` whose topic is a `SpellId` (`SPELLS`, `isSpell`). A spell has no cards: it casts with the one researched path `save.spellPaths` names (`equipSpell`, `decodeSpellPaths`; the first path researched is chosen automatically, unlearning clears it) at its furthest rank (`spellPath`, snapshotted into `Bonuses.necromancy` at battle start); the Study shows a Cast with picker above the tree. `defend/necromancy.ts`: every enemy death (not fortress parts) leaves a `sim.graves` entry with its difficulty for `NECRO.graveLife` (10) seconds times `Bonuses.graveTime` (Grave time: 20 s, then 30 s); `castNecromancy` (`sim.castNecromancy`, cooldown `necroRemaining`) raises each grave within `NECRO.radius` on open ground as a friendly `Soldier` of kind `"undead"` (`home` -1, `risen` set), up to `NECRO.maxRisen` alive, each crumbling after `NECRO.life` seconds without counting as a lost troop. Paths: Bone archers (range, harder arrows, twin shot), Soul weighing (strength by the fallen's difficulty, `SOUL_WEIGHT` caps) and Amalgam (one giant from the summed difficulty, cleave at II, never crumbles at III). Graves follow from deaths the replay already hashes, and nothing draws from the random stream, so runs without a cast replay unchanged. `necro-art.ts` draws the risen sprites, the cast's ring and wisps and its relief light; while it is held, `Overlay.graves` puts a black-outlined pixel skull on every grave on the board (`drawSkulls` in `edit-overlay.ts`, brighter over those the aim would raise), over the board or not. Run `tests/defend-necromancy.test.ts` and `node tests/defend-necromancy.mjs` (dev server required; isolated stress fixture, no player saves).

**Fire and ice cancel.** `defend/fire-ice.ts` (`quench`, after `stepBlazes`): where a blaze (fireball, Molten core crater, grenade) and Frost nova rime overlap, the smaller goes out in steam and the larger keeps the leftover area (radius √(R² − r²)); burning ground thaws enemies walking in it (not fliers or the sheltered), rime douses burning ones, and an enemy both burning and chilled or frozen loses both. **Developer controls.** `settings.developerMode` is the master Developer mode checkbox: dev options only render while checked; turning it off clears `DEV_OPTIONS` (including `devMode`, the separate unlimited-money flag). Saves predating the master switch with enabled cheats migrate it on; explicitly off saves clear cheats on decode. `DefendHost.devMode()` reports the master switch. In Developer mode, the Wave picker offers Unit tester. `DefendSim.startUnitTester()` disables automatic waves and clear events; `summonEnemy()` uses ordinary spawn/fortress/chain assembly, capped by live enemy count. Its Enemies sidebar lists every catalog kind with existing portraits; pointer presses summon immediately and, after 350 ms, repeat every 100 ms while held. Release, cancellation, blur, tab changes, sidebar closing and battle ending cancel the hold. Tester runs neither earn progression nor record journal discoveries and are never saved. Run `tests/defend-unit-tester.test.ts` and `node tests/defend-unit-tester.mjs` (dev server required; isolated saves).

**Expanded zone enemies.** Every themed roster has at least 15 species (Frozen Vault has 20); 37 native additions span Mossbound, Amber, Ember, Drowned, Fungal, Violet, Obsidian and Astral, also eligible in Nadir and affordable on their first zone visit. **Fortress stride polish.** `fortress-motion-art.ts` uses round limb caps/joins so the contour's wider stroke cannot grow acute knee miters into triangular spikes. Keep round joints when changing the gait. Legs have broader struts/feet, a longer reach and a 74% planted stance with a short knee settle. `fortress-footfall-art.ts` keeps cosmetic landing puffs in a bounded per-core WeakMap, detecting stride-boundary crossings from actual walk distance, interpolating ground contacts and expiring on battle time.
